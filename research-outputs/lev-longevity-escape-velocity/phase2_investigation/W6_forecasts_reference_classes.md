## 9. Limitations

1. **Budget exhaustion.** After Q-22 the tool refused further calls; a shared session budget (with parallel workstreams) is likely but not confirmed. About half of the must-answer material was never searched (section 2).
2. **Summary-mediated figures.** No primary text was read. Q-20 to Q-22 each returned several link lists from internal sub-searches; each is logged as one query, so at most F2 applies.
3. **Secondary-source dominance.** Twenty of 27 entries are V1 event-log items. Statement dates come from URLs, headlines or the summaries. Several domains are commercial (nmn.com, nad.com, aspriva.com, fatty15.com) and are treated as stake-bearing origins; echoes (for example a blog and its repost) count as one origin.
4. **Attribution gaps.** Several summary sentences name no source (the Kurzweil 86% figure, the "about 4 months" RMR1 figure, Diamandis's second-hand dates for Church and Sinclair); each is marked and none is used as a finding.
5. **No conversion.** No forecast is converted to a hazard rate. Every row is "not convertible" (no band or duration), and the alternative readings are listed in 3A.
6. **Query errors.** Two queries mis-worded a title or author list (Q-02, Q-17) and two carried guessed company names (Q-19) or a "seven years" figure (Q-18); the cards use only what the results show.
7. **Citation chaining** was not used (not executable here); pointers in section 6 come from result lists only.
8. **Same-family model.** The drafting agent and later reviewers share a model family; no cross-model check was run.
9. **Query-carried figures (added in Phase 5 revision 2).** Q-11 carried "early 2030s" and Q-20 carried "115 years"; the echoes in the outputs are not corroboration (the report marks them with a dagger).
10. **Frontier scope.** Nothing here applies beyond high-income frontier populations, and the vintage table is a dated snapshot of statements, not evidence about outcomes.
