## Conventions used in this file

- **Levels.** Existence E2/E1/E0 and figure F3/F2/F-P/F1 per Blueprint v2, tightened by amendment v2.1: F3 needs output sentences that attribute the figure to a named source, from separately worded queries, naming at least two sources of different authorship, at least one primary and independent of the claim's producer. **No card reaches V3.** Every ITP figure traces to papers by the ITP investigators (one authorship group); every RMR1 figure traces to the sponsor. Sponsor claims are V2-P.
- **Attribution.** Each card gives the output sentence that names the source. Where the sentence itself does not, the card says "by framing" and quotes the framing. Unattributed figures are listed in §6c and are not carded.
- **Title-level.** A claim visible only as a result title, with no figure in any output, is marked "title-level" and capped at F2.
- **Leading query.** Q-05 and Q-09 embedded the expected figure in the query text, and Q-11 embedded reversed sexes. They are flagged and never counted as corroboration. In both cases the output added details not in the query (p-values; a corrected venue; corrected sexes), which suggests the summary read the source, but that is not proof.
- **Tier convention.** `source_quality_hierarchy.md` defines design levels I to VII but not tiers 1 to 4. Used here: Tier 1 peer-reviewed article or official body page; Tier 2 preprint, registry or database record; Tier 3 foundation, company or institution web report; Tier 4 news, blog, forum, social post. Animal studies are not placed on the human I-VII ladder; the Blueprint's translational modifier ("multi-site replicated") is stated instead.
- **Link classes.** pub publisher; pm PubMed or PMC; reg registry; pre preprint; off official body or database; inst institution page or repository copy; cof company or foundation; news news or trade press; blog blog, forum or community site; agg aggregator (ResearchGate, H1 Connect, meta.org, DOAJ); soc social post; pat patent office; pr press release.
- **Independence applied.** Publisher, PMC, PubMed and repository copies of one article are one origin. Blog or news rewrites of one article or release add no independent origin.

## 5. Contradictions and counter-evidence

**Summary self-contradictions and cross-output conflicts**

- **C1. Rapamycin numbers (W3-02, W3-03).** Q-04 lists rapamycin at 14 ppm as +13.4% females and +19.6% males, and at 42 ppm as +14.9% females and +10.6% males, with no attribution. This conflicts with Q-03 (Miller 2014: +23% males, +26% females at three times the earlier dose, and females above males at every dose), with Q-02 (+14% females, +9% males at the age of 90% mortality) and with Q-04's own closing line that rapamycin is the only drug that works better in females. Unresolved; the Q-04 numbers are dropped. The metrics also differ (median versus 90th percentile).
- **C2. Counts of positive compounds (W3-01).** Q-01 says six compounds extended lifespan (rapamycin, 17aE2, NDGA, Protandim, glycine, acarbose) and then that five agents including aspirin and canagliflozin were male-only, although those two are not among the six. Q-04 and Q-06 give 54 tested and thirteen positive. Best read as source vintage (older versus 2025 counts); not resolved by a primary source.
- **C3. Per-site rapamycin values (W3-02).** Q-02 gives male mean gains of 5%, 8% and 15% at the three sites without naming a source; not repeated; not used.
- **C4. RMR1 additive versus synergistic (W3-10).** Q-14 says "additive (not synergistic)" and, two sentences later, that males "showed real synergy" when damage-repair interventions were stacked on rapamycin. Q-16 says male results were "messier" because of early deaths or late catch-ups. Unresolved.
- **C5. RMR1 status label (W3-10).** Q-18 presents "six of the 20 treatment groups ... extinct" and "25% survival at over 35 months" under "Final Cohort Status". The same sentence appears in the link title of de Grey's "August RMR1 update" X post (Q-14, which says the study was "fairly near to the end"), so it is an interim status, not the final one.
- **C6. mTERT figure (W3-10).** Q-15 states "Aged mice that resumed mTERT therapy lived to an average 37.5 months, a 41.4% increase over controls", with no attribution. It sits badly with "about 4 months" for the best group and may come from a different dataset or subgroup. Not used.
- **C7. RMR1 start age.** 18 months in Q-13, Q-16, Q-20; "approximately 19 months" in Q-14.
- **C8. RMR1 mean versus median.** Q-14 says mean lifespan was extended and gives the roughly 4 months as a median extension. Which statistic is meant: not resolved.
- **C9. Leading queries (W3-04, W3-06, W3-07).** Q-05, Q-09 and Q-11 embedded figures in the query. Q-09's output corrected the venue and Q-11's corrected the reversed sexes, and Q-05's output added p-values and 90th-percentile figures absent from the query, which is evidence of source reading but not independent corroboration.
- **C10. "More potent than either component alone" (W3-07).** Q-08 and Q-11 make this comparison against prior cohorts of rapamycin-only mice, not a concurrent factorial arm.

**Counter-evidence and balance for Phase 3 (reading notes; no weights assigned; not conversions)**

- Against a reading that mouse pharmacology already shows large, general, stackable gains: strong sex dimorphism (W3-01, W3-04, W3-05, W3-06); for males, in every ITP result here that gives both statistics the 90th-percentile gain is smaller than the median gain (acarbose 22% median versus 11%; canagliflozin 14% versus 9%; 17-alpha-estradiol from 16 months 19% versus 7%), although the one female case (acarbose: median 5%, 90th percentile 9%) goes the other way; and RMR1 reports no radical extension of maximum lifespan (W3-10).
- Against a reading that no intervention robustly extends mammalian lifespan: attributed multi-site results (canagliflozin at each of three sites, W3-06; the combination in both sexes at all three sites, W3-07).
- Not comparable across studies: RMR1 (C57BL/6J, start about 18 months, single site, sponsor-reported) versus ITP (UM-HET3, starts at 9 to 20 months, three sites, peer-reviewed).
- Translational caveat: all cards are mouse data; no card addresses human translation.
