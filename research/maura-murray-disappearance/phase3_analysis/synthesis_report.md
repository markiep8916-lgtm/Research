## Key Themes

### Theme 1: She left the car alive, functional, and intending to avoid the police

**Evidence Strength**: Strong
**Sources**: 8 sources, tiers 1 to 7 (nhdoj_victimpage; fbi_vicap2024; aetv2025; mediapressure_ep5; ap2019report; ap2024fox; tapatalk_marrotte; the107timeline)
**Synthesis**: The official record places a private citizen in conversation with her at the scene shortly before police arrived [nhdoj_victimpage]. The citizen's account, consistent across two decades of retellings, is that she was shaken and shivering but uninjured, declined help, said she had already called AAA, and asked him not to call the police [aetv2025; mediapressure_ep5]. There was no cell service at the site [aetv2025], so the AAA statement was false, which makes her request the more telling: she did not want police contact. The police report describes a locked car with both airbags deployed, a box of wine on the back seat and a strong odour of alcohol [ap2019report]; she had crashed her father's car two nights earlier [wikipedia_maura, reconstruction tier]. Neighbours reportedly saw her walking around the car with the hazard lights on [tapatalk_marrotte]. She took her wallet, cards, phone, and the liquor she had bought that afternoon, and left the textbooks, accident forms, jewellery and running gear [newsnation2024; truecrimesociety2023]. Between the citizen's 911 call at 7:42 PM and the officer's arrival at 7:45 or 7:46 PM she was gone [mediapressure_ep5; the107timeline]. Anchor justification: every element is attested by an official or mainstream source, with amateur sources adding only minute-level detail.

### Theme 2: The trail leads east along the road and then stops; the near-scene woods were checked

**Evidence Strength**: Moderate
**Sources**: 5 sources (globe2004vanishes; scarinza2004; nwp2019dogtrack; nwp2020searches; nhdoj_2022search)
**Synthesis**: The earliest mainstream account says the tracking dogs lost her scent within 100 feet of the accident and that searchers covering nearly 20 miles of Route 112 found no footprints in the snow [globe2004vanishes]. The search supervisor, as relayed by an independent blog, said the bloodhound was run twice from a glove on the morning of February 11, about 39 to 40 hours after the crash, headed east both times and stopped within sight of the scene, and that the possibility of a vehicle pickup at that point was noted [nwp2019dogtrack]. The lead investigator said in 2004 that police were reasonably confident she did not enter the woods near the scene, which had been searched several times [scarinza2004]. The end point of the track is reported five different ways (100 feet; 100 yards; under 600 feet; near a neighbour's driveway; the Bradley Hill Road intersection). Anchor justification: the direction (east) and the shortness of the track are consistent across every source; the distance is not.

### Theme 3: Searches were extensive but late, and terrain can hide a body for years

**Evidence Strength**: Moderate
**Sources**: 6 sources (nwp2020searches; scarinza2004; nhdoj_2022search; largay_nbc; largay_cbs; rothschild1995)
**Synthesis**: Five official searches ran in 2004 with dogs, ground teams and a FLIR helicopter, covering, in the supervisor's words, 12 miles of roadway and one to two miles into the woods [nwp2020searches]; a 90-person search followed in July 2004 [scarinza2004]; and in July 2022 the state ran a more extensive search of areas previously searched in a more limited fashion, off Route 112 in Landaff and Easton, about five miles east [nhdoj_2022search; indepthnh2022]. The first search began roughly 36 hours after the crash, which matters for the thermal imaging: a person who died of exposure on the first night would no longer emit a heat signature by the time the helicopter flew. The forensic literature shows hypothermia victims often end in concealed, protective positions [rothschild1995], and a well-documented Maine case shows a body two miles from a major trail going unfound for over two years despite an extensive search [largay_nbc; largay_cbs]. Anchor justification: the search facts come from official statements relayed by a blog and an archived press report; the interpretive point about FLIR timing is the synthesis's own and is labelled as such.

### Theme 4: The state has treated the case as a potential crime since 2007 without naming anyone, and keeps searching terrain

**Evidence Strength**: Strong
**Sources**: 9 sources (strelzin2007; concordmonitor2024photo; nhpr2017notreopened; unionleader2017lingers; bostonglobe2022vicap; concordmonitor2022; nhdoj_2019search; nhdoj_2022search; unionleader2026tech)
**Synthesis**: In 2007 the Attorney General's office said the state was treating the disappearance as a potential homicide and handling it as a criminal investigation [strelzin2007]; in 2009 it was labelled suspicious and assigned to the cold case unit [concordmonitor2024photo]; in 2017 the office said the case was never closed [nhpr2017notreopened] and that it still did not know what happened [unionleader2017lingers]; in 2022 it asked the FBI to enter the case into ViCAP, a violent-crime linkage system [concordmonitor2022]; in 2019 it dug, with the FBI, in a basement the family suspected, and found nothing [nhdoj_2019search; bostoncom2019]; in 2022 it searched woods five miles east on the strength of a 2004 witness report [nhdoj_2022search; indepthnh2022]; and in 2026 it said it was applying new technology to existing evidence [unionleader2026tech]. The investigators' revealed behaviour therefore straddles two families of explanation: violent crime by an unknown person, and death on foot in terrain. No official has said she is believed dead, and none has named a person of interest. Anchor justification: all items are official statements or official actions reported by mainstream outlets.

### Theme 5: The pre-trip behaviour is consistent with a planned, concealed, temporary absence

**Evidence Strength**: Moderate
**Sources**: 6 sources (bostonmag2014; fbi_vicap2024; ap2014decade; eonline2017; the107timeline; newsnation2024)
**Synthesis**: She told her professors of a death in the family that had not occurred [bostonmag2014], told no one of the trip [fbi_vicap2024], withdrew money and drove north [ap2014decade], and bought alcohol [aetv2025]. She also, per the independent reconstruction, called the owner of a condominium in Bartlett, New Hampshire, and a Stowe, Vermont tourism line that afternoon [the107timeline], which is lodging-seeking behaviour, and she left in the car the things a student expects to need on return [newsnation2024]. The pattern fits a person who wanted several days away, alone, without questions, better than it fits either a permanent departure or a resolved intention to die. Anchor justification: the deception and the withdrawal are mainstream-attested; the lodging calls are reconstruction-tier and are weighted accordingly.

### Priors from the reference classes

- Most missing adults are found quickly and alive [nij_lostnotforgotten; ukmpu2023]; intentional absence is common and short [biehal2003]. A case still open after 22 years is far into the tail, where the population is dominated by two outcomes: a body that was never found, and a crime that was never solved.
- Violence is documented in about 10% of NamUs cases [nij_violence]; that is a floor for this case, because undocumented violence is exactly what "no body, no offender" looks like.
- Despondent and intoxicated subjects are usually found close to the point last seen [dbs_despondent; koester2008]; exposure victims hide themselves [rothschild1995]; alcohol raises the risk [freund1994; alcalc1984]. A body in a river, a culvert, a hollow or dense cover near a road can go unfound for years [largay_nbc].
- Veteran bloodhounds work 48-hour trails reliably in controlled tests [harvey2003], but a trail ending on a plowed road after 40 hours of traffic is weaker evidence of a vehicle than a trail ending on soil.

Judgmental prior ranges before the case-specific evidence, for a distressed, drinking young adult who leaves a disabled car on a rural winter road at night and is not found within days: on-foot death (H1 plus H2) 45 to 60%; third-party harm (H3) 20 to 35%; companion (H5) 5 to 10%; voluntary permanent disappearance (H4) 5 to 10%; official involvement (H6) 1 to 3%.

## Theoretical Integration

The analysis follows ACH [heuer1999] with the correction for vague hypotheses, uses reference-class priors from search-and-rescue and missing-person research, and applies the suite's tier discipline so that the amateur reconstructions that dominate the online record supply detail but never carry a conclusion. The contamination of the record itself [yardley2018; crimecon_mmm] is treated as a finding, which is why the report leads with what the official and contemporaneous sources establish.

## Synthesis Limitations

- Snippet-only evidence; no full text; attributions probable rather than confirmed for several quotations.
- The ranges are the synthesis's judgment, not a computed posterior; the matrix is exposed so a reader can re-weight.
- H1 and H2 cannot be separated by outcome evidence, only by pre-event evidence; the split between them is the least reliable number in the table.
- H3's low inconsistency is partly a property of its vagueness; the positive-evidence requirement was applied but is itself a judgment.
- Base-rate populations only partly resemble this case.
