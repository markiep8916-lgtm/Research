## Major Issues

1. **H3 wins partly by being vague**
   - **Type**: Logical fallacy (unfalsifiability) / Method
   - **Location**: ACH tally and ranking
   - **Problem**: "Killed by an unknown third party" is consistent with nearly any evidence pattern, so its low inconsistency tally is not the same kind of achievement as H1's. The synthesis acknowledges this and applies a positive-evidence requirement, but the positive items it cites (track ending on a road; the state's posture; no remains) are each also consistent with H1 in a plausible reading.
   - **Impact**: The margin between H3 and H1 could be overstated.
   - **Recommendation**: In the report, present H3 and H1 as the two live explanations, state the ranking as conditional, name the two structural judgments the order depends on, and give H3's sub-variants with the observations that would falsify each. Do not present a single figure without its range.

2. **"No remains in 22 years" and "searches found nothing" are not independent**
   - **Type**: Evidence (double counting)
   - **Location**: E11 and E19
   - **Problem**: Both items count against H1 and H2. They are related: a body missed by the 2004 searches is also a body not found since. Counting both at full weight would double-penalize the on-foot hypotheses.
   - **Recommendation**: Keep them as separate rows because they measure different things (organized search coverage versus chance discovery over two decades), but weight E11 at half, as the synthesis already does, and say so in the report.

## Observations

- The strongest single fact for H1 that the online debate underweights is the FLIR timing: a helicopter that flies 36 hours after a winter night cannot detect a person who died on that night. The report should make this explicit, with the caveat that ground teams and dogs do not depend on heat.
- The strongest single fact for H3 that the online debate overweights is the dog track: a scent trail lost on a plowed, trafficked road after 40 hours is a weak vehicle indicator, as the search supervisor's own relayed remarks concede.
- The state's 2022 search five miles east, based on the 2004 report, is the clearest public signal that investigators had not excluded an on-foot journey east as of 2022. The report should say this plainly rather than let the ViCAP request stand alone as the state's revealed belief.
- Nothing in the record requires a local perpetrator specifically; the family's "local" framing is a belief, and the report should keep H3a (passing motorist of any origin) as the most plausible sub-variant without adopting the "local" qualifier.
