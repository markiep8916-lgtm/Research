# Amyloid-β: What It Does, and Whether It Can Be Cleared Without Sleep

**Type:** Quick evidence review (web-sourced), not a systematic review
**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Companion to:** [`sleep-need-and-reduction.md`](sleep-need-and-reduction.md)
**Question:** What is amyloid-β (Aβ) for? How does the brain remove it? Can it be removed without sleep, and would that let us rely less on sleep?

---

## Bottom line

1. **Aβ is not simply waste.** At normal (picomolar) levels it supports synaptic plasticity and memory and acts as an antimicrobial peptide. Suppressing its *production* in humans made cognition **worse** (BACE-inhibitor trials). The goal is balance, not elimination.
2. **Sleep is only one of several clearance routes.** Aβ is also broken down by enzymes, carried across the blood-brain barrier (LRP1), eaten by microglia, and drained through meningeal lymphatics. Sleep mainly affects the *fluid-flow* (glymphatic) route and *lowers production*, because neurons release less Aβ when less active.
3. **Some sleep-independent methods do remove Aβ:**
   - **Anti-amyloid antibodies** (lecanemab, donanemab) are approved and clear plaques in humans, with modest clinical benefit and real brain-swelling/bleeding risk.
   - **Plasma exchange** (AMBAR trial) showed mixed but encouraging results.
   - **40 Hz light/sound stimulation, exercise, focused ultrasound, and lymphatic-targeted approaches** are at the mouse, pilot or early-trial stage.
4. **Clearing Aβ without sleep would not remove the need for sleep.** Aβ is one of many things sleep deals with (see the companion report: memory consolidation, synaptic renormalisation, immune and hormonal regulation). People on anti-amyloid drugs still need normal sleep. The idea that "sleep exists to wash out Aβ" is itself now **contested** (§3).

## 1. What amyloid-β is and what it does normally

Aβ is a 38–43 amino-acid peptide cut from amyloid precursor protein (APP) by β-secretase (BACE1) and γ-secretase. Neurons release it in proportion to their **activity**, so more is made during wakefulness ([Kang et al. 2009, *Science*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2789838/)).

| Physiological role | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Synaptic plasticity and memory** | At picomolar levels Aβ *enhances* long-term potentiation in the hippocampus and supports learning in animals. Removing endogenous Aβ causes synaptic dysfunction, while excess causes hyperexcitability (an inverted-U dose response) | [PMC10387033 review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10387033/); [Frontiers 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/molecular-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnmol.2020.577622/full) |
| **Antimicrobial defence** | Aβ kills 8 common pathogens with potency equal to or greater than the classic human antimicrobial peptide LL-37. Its oligomers form fibrils that trap microbes | [Soscia et al. 2010, *PLOS One*](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0009505); [review 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29504537/) |
| **Other proposed roles** | Angiogenesis, tumour suppression, recovery from injury, neurogenesis, glutamate recycling | [J Nutr Health Aging review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12603-019-1162-5) |

**Why this matters for "elimination":** Two BACE1 inhibitors that cut Aβ production (verubecestat, atabecestat) caused dose-dependent cognitive worsening and more depression, anxiety and sleep disturbance. The effects reversed about 6 months after stopping ([*Alzheimer's & Dementia* 2020](https://alz-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/alz.12164); [Alzforum](https://www.alzforum.org/news/conference-coverage/bump-road-or-disaster-bace-inhibitors-worsen-cognition)). BACE1 also cuts many other substrates, so this does not prove the harm came from missing Aβ. It does show that pushing Aβ toward zero is not a safe goal.

Aβ becomes harmful when production exceeds clearance over years. It then forms soluble oligomers and plaques, which are a hallmark of Alzheimer's disease.

## 2. How the brain clears Aβ (and which routes depend on sleep)

| Route | Mechanism | Sleep-dependent? |
|---|---|---|
| **Enzymatic breakdown** | Neprilysin, insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) and endothelin-converting enzyme break Aβ down in the brain's fluid | Not primarily |
| **Transport across the blood-brain barrier** | LRP1 on the lining of brain blood vessels carries Aβ into the blood. LRP1 **declines with age** | Not primarily |
| **Microglia and astrocytes** | Uptake and lysosomal breakdown (microglial LRP1 plays a major role) | Partly (immune and inflammatory state) |
| **Glymphatic / perivascular flow** | Cerebrospinal fluid flows along arteries, mixes with brain fluid through aquaporin-4 (AQP4) water channels on astrocytes, and drains along veins | **Yes, reportedly increased in sleep** |
| **Meningeal and cervical lymphatics** | Drain cerebrospinal fluid and solutes to the deep cervical lymph nodes. Function declines with age | Indirectly (fed by glymphatic flow) |
| **Peripheral sink** | Aβ in the blood (~90–95% bound to albumin) is removed by the liver and kidneys, keeping the brain-to-blood gradient | No |

Sources: [Clearance pathways editorial (PMC5663857)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5663857/); [CAA clearance review (PMC5559841)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5559841/); [Neural Regen Res 2025 systematic review](https://journals.lww.com/nrronline/fulltext/2025/12000/systematic_review_of_amyloid_beta_clearance.27.aspx); [LRP1 in microglia (PMC3846796)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3846796/). Published reviews do not give reliable percentages for each route's share.

**Sleep affects Aβ in two separate ways:**
- **Less production.** Aβ in brain fluid rises with wakefulness and with orexin infusion, and falls with an orexin-blocking drug ([Kang 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2789838/)). A lumbar-catheter study found the same daily rhythm in human cerebrospinal fluid.
- **More clearance.** In mice, sleep enlarged the space between brain cells by ~60% and sped up Aβ clearance ([Xie et al. 2013, *Science*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3880190/)).

**Human evidence linking sleep loss to Aβ:**
- One night without sleep raised hippocampal and thalamic Aβ on PET by ~5% (n = 20) ([Shokri-Kojori et al. 2018, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1721694115)).
- One night without sleep reduced clearance of an MRI tracer injected into the spinal fluid ([Eide et al. 2021, *Brain*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33829232/)).
- In a 39-person randomised crossover trial, morning **plasma** Aβ and tau were higher after normal sleep than after sleep deprivation. The authors read this as sleep-driven glymphatic flushing into the blood ([*Nat Commun* 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68374-8); [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.07.30.24311248.full.pdf)).

## 3. Contradiction: does sleep actually speed clearance?

[Miao et al. 2024, *Nature Neuroscience*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-024-01638-y) (Franks and Wisden labs) used a different tracer method in mice. They found fluid movement in the brain was **independent of sleep/wake state**, and that clearance was **reduced** during sleep and anaesthesia. The authors state their results "challenge the idea that the core function of sleep is to clear toxins from the brain" ([UK DRI](https://www.ukdri.ac.uk/news-and-events/scientists-find-sleep-may-not-clear-brain-toxins-as-previously-thought); [Alzforum debate](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/does-sleep-really-speed-clearance-brain); [The Transmitter](https://www.thetransmitter.org/glymphatic-system/new-method-reignites-controversy-over-brain-clearance-during-sleep/)).

**How to weigh it:** Xie 2013 and Miao 2024 are both mouse studies using different methods. The later human studies (Eide 2021; *Nat Commun* 2026) support sleep-linked clearance, but they are indirect: tracer MRI and plasma biomarkers, not direct flow measurements. Whatever the clearance picture, **less production during sleep is not disputed.** Honest summary: sleep lowers net Aβ build-up. How much of that comes from clearance rather than reduced production is still unresolved.

| **Plasma exchange with albumin** (AMBAR) | Peripheral sink | Randomised phase 2b/3 trial, n = 347: ADCS-ADL 52% less decline (p = .03); ADAS-Cog 66% less (p = .06, not significant); moderate-stage subgroup 61% less decline on both ([Boada et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7984263/)). ## 5. Would clearing Aβ without sleep let us sleep less?

**No, based on current evidence.** Four reasons:

1. **Aβ is one waste product among many.** Sleep is also tied to tau clearance, memory consolidation, synaptic downscaling, and immune and endocrine regulation (companion report). Removing one metabolite does not stand in for the others.
2. **No sleep-independent method has been tested for whether it reduces sleep need.** Not antibodies, not plasma exchange, not 40 Hz stimulation. Anti-amyloid trials measured decline, not sleep.
3. **Treated patients still show the full effects of sleep loss.** Cognitive impairment from short sleep appears within days (Van Dongen 2003, companion report). That is far faster than Aβ builds up, so Aβ cannot be the main thing driving how impaired you feel.
4. **Bidirectional risk.** Poor sleep raises Aβ, and Aβ pathology disrupts sleep. The best-supported approach is to **stack** protections (good sleep, exercise, blood-pressure and cardiovascular health), not to swap one for another.

**Practical, evidence-supported steps for keeping Aβ in check (for healthy adults):**
- Regular sleep of 7 or more hours (lowers production and, likely, improves clearance).
- Regular aerobic exercise (supports glymphatic and lymphatic flow and improves sleep).
- Treat sleep apnoea and look after cardiovascular health (arterial pulsation helps drive perivascular flow; mechanism evidence).
- Antibody therapies and plasma exchange are for diagnosed early Alzheimer's under specialist care, not for enhancement.

## 6. Evidence quality and gaps

- **Strong:** anti-amyloid antibody phase 3 trials (plaque removal and modest clinical effect); BACE-inhibitor harms; Aβ rising with sleep deprivation in humans (PET, cerebrospinal fluid).
- **Moderate:** AMBAR (one trial, partially significant endpoints); human exercise-glymphatic imaging; human sleep-glymphatic tracer and plasma studies.
- **Preliminary:** 40 Hz stimulation (mouse), FUS (n = 3), VEGF-C and LRP1 (mouse), neck lymphatic surgery (suspended).
- **Contradiction disclosed:** Xie 2013 / Nedergaard-lab glymphatic model (clearance ↑ in sleep) vs. Miao et al. 2024 (clearance ↓ in sleep). Unresolved; human data leans toward sleep-linked clearance.
- **Gap:** No study has tested whether any Aβ-clearing intervention changes how much sleep humans need or how impaired they get without it.
