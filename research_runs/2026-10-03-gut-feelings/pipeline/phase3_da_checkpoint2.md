### Major Issues

**M1. Theme 5 is labelled "Strong" in violation of the report's own convention, and its "four independent traditions" are citation-chained.** Confidence 0.85.
The convention at the head of the report defines Strong as "≥1 Level I/II source with independent replication or a pre-registered large-N test, converging with other levels." Theme 5's seven sources are Levels IV–VII (one Level IV field study, one Level V review, five Level VII position papers/monographs); the synthesis itself concedes "no RCT located" and "untested as an intervention at Level I–II" (Knowledge Gap 2). "Strong by convergence" is therefore a label the convention does not permit. The independence claim is also weaker than stated: the SQ2 bibliography records that Kahneman & Klein (2009) adopt Hogarth's kind/wicked distinction and rely on Shanteau's domain lists; that Hogarth 2015's co-author Lejarraga is first author of Lejarraga 2021; and that Kahneman & Klein's validity criterion "is partly circular unless operationalised." Four traditions that cite one another and share authors are convergent, not independent. This matters because Contradictions 6, 7 and 10 and the whole Theoretical Integration are "resolved by Theme 5": a post-hoc classifier (call any failure "wicked") is doing the work of a tested predictor. This is appeal to authority at the exact point where the report's answer to the primary RQ rests.

**M2. Construct conflation the definitional table was built to prevent: Theme 6/Contradiction 7 and Themes 5/9 use deliberate-judgment evidence as evidence about gut feelings.** Confidence 0.85.
Contradiction 6 dissolves ambady1992thin vs grove2000clinical/kuncel2013mechanical because they measure "different constructs" (cue detection vs holistic deliberate combination). Contradiction 7 then resolves dane2012trust vs kuncel2013mechanical/grove2000clinical with "Level I wins where they overlap" — but by the synthesis's own Theme 8 and Synthesis Limitation 4 they do not overlap: Grove/Kuncel "bound human judgment broadly, not gut feeling narrowly." Theme 6's sentence "Two Level I meta-analyses mark the boundary" of expertise-based intuition is therefore the conflation the table forbids. The same error recurs with tetlock2005expert: the SQ2 annotation states "forecasts were deliberate, not strictly 'gut' judgments, so it bounds expert judgment broadly rather than intuition narrowly," yet Theme 5 presents it as "the field demonstration" of intuitive failure and Theme 9 as evidence that gut-feeling confidence is non-diagnostic, with no caveat in either place. Remove the conflated sources and SQ2's "against intuition in wicked environments" column has no Level I–IV source that measured a gut feeling.

**M3. "Confidence, vividness and the feeling of knowing are non-diagnostic of accuracy" (Theme 9) and "The strength of the feeling says nothing about which case you are in" (plain-language answer) overstate the corpus and contradict Theme 4.** Confidence 0.8.
koriat1993how, as annotated, says FOK "is accurate to the extent that accessible partial information is itself correct — explaining both FOK validity and its illusions"; its direction is "conditional," not "against." lufityanto2016measuring reports that nonconscious emotional information "boost[ed] accuracy and confidence" together, i.e., confidence tracked accuracy. Theme 4 itself states the correct position: diagnosticity "is an empirical property of the situation." Theme 9's heading, its closing line ("information about the judge, not the world"), and the plain-language "says nothing" convert a conditional into an absolute. The honest claim is "not reliably diagnostic across environments; the confidence–accuracy relation is itself a property of the learning environment."

**M4. Physiological presentiment is treated as explained away, not as untested on the Phase 1 standard.** Confidence 0.8.
The plain-language answer says presentiment "has a demonstrated ordinary explanation (the body tracks the pattern of what just happened)." The SQ3 annotation of siller2015investigating states the authors' own limitation: the design "departs from classical presentiment protocols, so (as the authors state) it cannot settle whether earlier positive findings were expectation confounds"; it is also not pre-registered. "Demonstrated" is therefore unsupported; "shown in principle" (Knowledge Gap 5's own wording) is the ceiling. The "Moderate (against)" strength for presentiment rests on one Level II non-pre-registered experiment plus a Level VII opinion (schwarzkopf2014should, Grade C). Meanwhile Duggan & Tressoldi (2018; 27 new experiments, ES = 0.28) is held back for cap reasons and Synthesis Limitation 5 asserts "None is expected to reverse a theme-level conclusion" — an assertion with no stated basis for this row, where the verdict is only Moderate. Note also the row is internally inconsistent: Contradiction 4 says "Partially resolved (against); gap recorded," which is right; the plain-language text says "demonstrated," which is not.

**M5. The Bem-paradigm verdict generalises from three experiments to nine, calls the proponent evidence "author-dependent" when the corpus contains independent-investigator estimates, and does not engage the proponents' replies.** Confidence 0.75.
(a) Theme 10's strength line reads "Strong (against) for the Bem paradigm," while the adjudication paragraph concedes the mega-replications tested Experiment 1 only and Ritchie/Galak cover Experiments 8–9. Experiments 2–7 (priming, habituation, approach/avoidance) have no located replication in the corpus; muhmenthaler2024 ("three Bem paradigms, >2,000 participants, no effect") is held back under the cap although it would widen coverage. The strength label should be scoped to the three tested paradigms. (b) The theme title says proponent effects are "author-dependent," yet bem2016feeling's annotation reports independent-investigator replications at g = 0.06, z = 4.16 across 33 labs. The right descriptor is "proponent-curated and non-pre-registered," not "author-dependent." (c) The annotations record three proponent counter-arguments the synthesis never states: experimenter-scepticism moderators (galak2012correcting, bem2016feeling notes), "psi-conducive conditions were absent" (kekecs2023raising notes), and the knowledge-based prior (Bem, Utts & Johnson 2011, held back). Omitting them is mild straw-manning — the adjudication reads as if proponents had no reply. The replies can be answered from the corpus (Kekecs was co-designed with proponent Tressoldi on the consensus panel; the experimenter-effect explanation makes the claim immune to independent replication), but the answer has to be given, not assumed.

**M6. The Remmers 2020 fluency null is over-read: an underpowered absence of evidence is allowed to outrank eleven experiments because it is pre-registered.** Confidence 0.7.
The annotation says fluency "did not significantly drive coherence judgments *in patients*" (N = 35 + 35, clinical sample). Theme 3 drops "in patients" and Contradiction 9 rules "Pre-registration favours Remmers on the fluency null." Pre-registration protects against false positives; it does not supply power, and a non-significant effect in a small clinical sample is not evidence against a fluency route demonstrated in 11 randomised experiments (topolinski2009architecture) and "replicated in multiple labs" per its annotation. The defensible statement is: the affect route has pre-registered support; the fluency route has no pre-registered test and one small null. The Evidence Convergence Map's "affect better supported" should be reworded accordingly.

**M7. Contradiction 1 (Bechara vs Maia & McClelland) is resolved on grounds the bibliography does not support.** Confidence 0.7.
Theme 2: "Resolving by design: the sensitive probes and larger N beat the coarse probes and N = 16." The verification report gives Maia & McClelland N ≈ 20 — not meaningfully larger than 16 — and the SQ1 annotation records that "reactivity of frequent probing [is] debated (Bechara et al. 2005 reply)" and that Maia had "no psychophysiology." The synthesis cites neither. The resolution direction is probably right (the Dunn 2006 review and later sensitive-probe replications support it), but the stated reasoning ("larger N") is false on the record and the live counter-argument (probing creates the explicit knowledge it measures) is omitted. State the real grounds.

**M8. Asymmetric critique sampling: pro-intuition and proponent results are paired with their refutations; sceptical Level I results are not paired with theirs.** Confidence 0.65.
Themes 2, 7 and 10 each follow "famous claim → its critics → its failed replication." No sceptical anchor receives the same treatment: grove2000clinical and kuncel2013mechanical appear without the standard objections (task representativeness, criterion contamination, the "broken-leg" case); nieuwenstein2015making's annotation notes the UTT objection that the normative/decomposable criterion "is not where unconscious thought should excel," which Theme 7 omits; tetlock2005expert appears without the author's own later work on trainable forecasting. This is direction bias of the kind Checkpoint 1 warned about, pointing the other way. The corpus was seed-driven by controversy, so the asymmetry is partly a sampling artefact — but the synthesis should say so rather than present the sceptical findings as uncontested.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

"The synthesis's answer to the primary RQ — gut feelings are accurate when, and only when, they are the output of learning in a kind, feedback-rich environment — is a Level V–VII consensus that has never been tested as a prediction. Every SQ2 contradiction is 'resolved by Theme 5', but Theme 5 classifies environments after the outcome is known: forecasting failed, so forecasting is wicked; chess succeeded, so chess is kind. Kahneman & Klein's own annotation calls the criterion 'partly circular unless operationalised.' Strip the sources that measured deliberate judgment rather than gut feelings (Grove, Kuncel, Tetlock) and the Grade C primary study (Dane 2012), and SQ2 contains no Level I–III evidence that a *gut feeling specifically* is more accurate in a kind environment than in a wicked one. What remains is a plausible framework, a thin-slice meta-analysis, and a null for unconscious thought — which is consistent with the sceptic's simpler story: fast judgments are accurate exactly when the underlying knowledge is accurate, and 'environment kindness' is a description of that knowledge's provenance, not an additional mechanism."

The synthesis can survive this (Hogarth 2015 does give an ex ante operationalisation — learning/prediction-setting match — and Lejarraga 2021 applies it at the literature level), but it must state the circularity objection, show the ex ante operationalisation, and downgrade the strength label (M1).

### What's Missing

1. **A Level I–II test of the master boundary condition** (an experiment or meta-analysis in which environment kindness is manipulated or coded ex ante and intuitive accuracy is the outcome). The corpus has none; the synthesis says so, then labels the theme Strong.
2. **Primary-level evidence on expert intuition accuracy** from the naturalistic-decision-making field literatures (fireground, nursing, chess, sport officiating). SQ2 represents NDM through two reviews (salas2010expertise, Grade C; kahneman2009conditions) and one Grade C experiment. Follow-up search targets: field or quasi-experimental studies with objective criteria; meta-analyses of expert–novice intuitive accuracy; the Good Judgment / forecasting-training literature as the counterweight to Tetlock 2005.
3. **Meta-analyses in SQ1** (IGT performance in healthy adults; implicit-learning paradigms; coherence-judgment accuracy). The bibliography attributes their absence to PsycINFO/Scopus being unreachable, not to the literature.
4. **The three failed seeds, and what they would change.** Betsch & Glöckner (2010; intuition as parallel-constraint-satisfaction that integrates large amounts of information without effort) directly challenges Theme 8's "the gut can be a good cue detector and a poor cue combiner" — with it, the clinical-vs-mechanical result would have to be reconciled with a proponent theory of intuitive *integration*, not dissolved by construct separation. Reber (1989) would give Theme 1 primary implicit-learning evidence (artificial-grammar learning) against the Shanks constraint, so the "substrate" claim would rest on less than reviews alone. Bowers et al. (1990) would supply the founding Level II coherence data to Theme 3, making the "above-chance coherence detection" claim rest on an original plus replications rather than replications alone. Net: the two SQ1/SQ2 themes most favourable to intuition would get stronger, and Theme 8's reconciliation would get harder.
5. **Held-back SQ3 items that bear on the contested rows:** Duggan & Tressoldi 2018 (presentiment update, ES = 0.28); Muhmenthaler et al. 2024 (three Bem paradigms, >2,000, null); Bem, Utts & Johnson 2011 (prior reply); Francis 2012 (excess-success test).
