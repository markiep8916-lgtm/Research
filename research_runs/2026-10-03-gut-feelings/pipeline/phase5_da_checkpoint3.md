**M1. The AI disclosure contradicts the sentence beneath it and overstates verification.** Confidence 0.9.

**M2. The Conclusion and plain-language answer assert a frequency the corpus does not measure: "Gut feelings are real, frequently accurate" (§6.1) and "real, often accurate" (§6.2).** Confidence 0.85.
No corpus source estimates how often gut feelings are accurate. The draft's own §4.5 states that at the primary level the pro-intuition evidence "reduces to ambady1992thin, dane2012trust (Grade C), wilson1991thinking (small, unreplicated) and lufityanto2016measuring (unreplicated)" and that "the pro-intuition case is framework-weighted"; §5.1 says the answer is "a well-supported framework, not a finding". "Frequently"/"often" is a base-rate claim, it is the first thing a reader of the Conclusion sees, and it moves in the pro-intuition direction that Checkpoint 1 warned about. The defensible sentence is "Gut feelings are real, can be accurate under identifiable conditions, and are explicable without a sixth sense." The same paragraph also says feedback and domain knowledge "are the only levers the evidence supports": the corpus contains no intervention study (Theme 5: "has not been tested as a prediction"; Knowledge Gap: untested at Level I–II), so the evidence supports no lever as a tested intervention. §5.2's bounding sentence ("Every recommendation here is bounded by evidence that is framework-level...") is correct and must be carried into §6.1, which currently drops it.

### Strongest Counter-Argument (and whether the draft answers it)

"The report's answer to its own primary research question is not an empirical answer. It says gut feelings are accurate when they are the output of learning under valid feedback in a regular environment; it then concedes (§5.1) that on this corpus that framework cannot be distinguished from the sceptic's redescription that fast judgments are accurate exactly when the underlying knowledge is accurate, that no study coded environment kindness ex ante and measured gut-feeling accuracy as the outcome, and that removing Level V–VII sources leaves only four evidence themes, three of which are negative (UTA null, clinical-versus-mechanical, Bem null) and one of which (thin slices) is a 1992 meta-analysis with consensus criteria. The draft engages this counter-argument more honestly than any pipeline draft I have reviewed: §5.1 states the sceptic's story in the sceptic's words, concedes indistinguishability, and names the ex ante operationalisation (Hogarth et al., 2015) as the way to distinguish them; §4.5 states plainly that the pro-intuition primary case is thin; Theme 5 and Theme 6 both say "a prediction, not a finding". With M2 fixed, the counter-argument is answered as well as this corpus allows: the report's contribution is the construct separation and the demonstration that the negative results and the positive framework are about different constructs, and it says so.
