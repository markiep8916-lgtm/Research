### Construct Definitions

The Phase 1 Devil's Advocate required the constructs covered by "intuition" and "gut feeling" to be separated before evidence is weighed. Every theme below names its construct, and where a source measured a *neighbouring* construct (deliberate holistic expert judgment; coded nonverbal behaviour) the text says so at the point of use. The last column states what each construct predicts about *when* the judgment will be accurate, which is the primary research question.

| Construct | Working definition (corpus-based) | Exemplar sources | Phenomenology | Accuracy prediction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Implicit learning / expert pattern recognition** | Rapid, nonconscious, holistic association over regularities acquired through experience: the "cognitive substrate of social intuition" (Lieberman, 2000 <!--ref:lieberman2000intuition-->); the working definition of Dane and Pratt (2007) <!--ref:dane2007exploring--> | lieberman2000intuition; dane2007exploring; hodgkinson2008intuition; kahneman2009conditions; salas2010expertise; shanks1994characteristics (constraint) | "I just know this is right"; basis not reportable | Accurate when the environment had learnable regularities and the learner received valid feedback; otherwise confidently wrong (Kahneman and Klein, 2009 <!--ref:kahneman2009conditions-->) |

The first three constructs share a phenomenology (a judgment arrives, its basis does not) but differ in mechanism and therefore in accuracy conditions. Only the fifth makes a prediction independent of prior learning, which is why its evidentiary standard differs in kind. The sixth row is added after Checkpoint 2 so that the Level I–IV sources on deliberate judgment cannot be silently read as gut-feeling evidence.

### Key Themes

#### Theme 1: Implicit learning and knowledge-based pattern recognition are the default explanation of accurate gut feelings; the "unconscious" part remains contested

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate · **Sources:** 9 (Levels V–VII; several from the same programmes) — lieberman2000intuition, dane2007exploring, hodgkinson2008intuition, kahneman2009conditions, shanks1994characteristics, salas2010expertise, norman2017causes, norman2024dual, cowey2010blindsight

**Synthesis.** Reviews and position papers converge — though the convergence is partly within programmes (Norman 2017/2024 are one group; Hodgkinson 2008 and 2018 share authors) — on the view that most accurate gut feelings are learned regularities retrieved without reportable reasoning. Lieberman (2000) <!--ref:lieberman2000intuition--> grounds this in implicit learning and basal-ganglia circuitry; Dane and Pratt (2007) <!--ref:dane2007exploring--> make effectiveness conditional on domain knowledge and learning history; Hodgkinson et al. (2008) <!--ref:hodgkinson2008intuition--> place the construct inside dual-process theory while separating it from insight; Salas et al. (2010) <!--ref:salas2010expertise--> distinguish expertise-based from naive intuition and locate its origin in feedback-rich practice. The clinical-reasoning programme turns this into an outcome claim: error falls with knowledge and instructions to slow down and be systematic do not improve accuracy (Norman et al., 2017 <!--ref:norman2017causes-->), and diagnostic errors "arise from lack of access to the appropriate knowledge, not from errors of processing" (Norman et al., 2024 <!--ref:norman2024dual-->). The mechanism predicts accuracy when regularities exist, were learned with valid feedback, and the current case belongs to the learned distribution — the conditions Kahneman and Klein (2009) <!--ref:kahneman2009conditions--> state for intuitive expertise, so Themes 1 and 5 describe one account.

The constraint is Shanks and St. John (1994) <!--ref:shanks1994characteristics-->: by their information and sensitivity criteria, unconscious learning "has not been satisfactorily established." The verification report notes this strong conclusion is no longer consensus, but the criteria still apply: much "intuitive" knowledge may be fragmentary and partly reportable rather than wholly nonconscious. Blindsight is the cleanest existence proof that useful knowledge can exist without awareness — patients "detect, localise and even discriminate visual stimuli that they deny seeing" (Cowey, 2010 <!--ref:cowey2010blindsight-->) — yet the same review catalogues artefacts and leaves everyday usefulness open. Moderate because the account rests on reviews; the corpus has no primary implicit-learning study (Reber 1989 failed anchor retrieval) and no Level I synthesis of expert-intuition accuracy. All SQ1 empirical sources use Western, mostly student, samples, so the cross-cultural generality of every mechanism theme is unknown.

#### Theme 2: Somatic and interoceptive signals modulate intuitive choice, but their causal, nonconscious status is not established and their measurement is unstable

**Evidence Strength:** Emerging (contested) · **Sources:** 7 (Levels I–V) — bechara1997deciding, maia2004reexamination, dunn2006somatic, dunn2010listening, kandasamy2016interoceptive, lufityanto2016measuring, hickman2020relationship

**Synthesis.** The somatic marker hypothesis is the most literal model of a gut feeling: "normals began to choose advantageously before they realized which strategy worked best," with anticipatory skin-conductance responses absent in ventromedial patients (Bechara et al., 1997 <!--ref:bechara1997deciding-->; 10 controls, 6 patients). The "before knowing" step did not survive scrutiny. With trial-by-trial quantitative probes, "participants report knowledge of the advantageous strategy more reliably than they behave advantageously" (Maia and McClelland, 2004 <!--ref:maia2004reexamination-->; N ≈ 20, no psychophysiology), and Dunn et al. (2006) <!--ref:dunn2006somatic--> found the IGT evidence undermined by "the cognitive penetrability of the reward/punishment schedule, ambiguity surrounding interpretation of the psychophysiological data, and a shortage of causal evidence." The samples are comparably small, so the resolution does not rest on N. It rests on three grounds: Maia's probes are the more sensitive awareness measure; the finding that knowledge accompanies advantageous choice was replicated in later sensitive-probe studies (per the annotation); and Dunn 2006 shows no causal test of peripheral feedback exists. The live counter-argument — that frequent probing creates the explicit knowledge it measures (Bechara et al. 2005 reply, recorded in the annotation) — would at most restore uncertainty about *when* knowledge becomes explicit; it cannot restore the positive claim, because nonconscious *guidance* was never shown causally. Bodily *co-variation* with choice stands.

Later work refines rather than rescues. "Increasing interoception ability either helped or hindered adaptive intuitive decision making, depending on whether the anticipatory bodily signals generated favored advantageous or disadvantageous choices" (Dunn et al., 2010 <!--ref:dunn2010listening-->): the body is an amplifier, not an oracle, so the mechanism predicts accuracy only when the signal was calibrated by valid outcomes. Kandasamy et al. (2016) <!--ref:kandasamy2016interoceptive--> supply the one field result — traders' interoceptive accuracy predicted profitability — but at N = 18, cross-sectionally and unreplicated, it is an ecological illustration only (Grade C). Lufityanto et al. (2016) <!--ref:lufityanto2016measuring--> show nonconscious emotional information can "boost accuracy and confidence in a concurrent emotion-free decision task," but only when valence was predictively paired with the correct answer — itself a learned contingency — and no independent replication was located. Finally, the two standard cardiac interoceptive-accuracy tasks show only "a small relationship" across 22 studies (Hickman et al., 2020 <!--ref:hickman2020relationship-->), so claims built on one task cannot be assumed on the other. No 2018–2026 Level I–II synthesis links interoceptive accuracy to adult decision quality.

#### Theme 3: Coherence intuitions are fast and above chance, and their experiential basis (fluency plus affect) is exactly what makes them hijackable

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate (Level II, replicated across three *interlinked* lab lines; no pre-registered test of the above-chance effect itself) · **Sources:** 6 (Levels II–VII) — bolte2005speed, topolinski2009architecture, horr2014feeling, zander2016intuition, remmers2020intuitive, koriat1993how

**Synthesis.** The coherence-judgment paradigm is the best-controlled laboratory model of knowing without knowing how. Participants "discriminated coherent and incoherent triads reliably better than chance, even when they did not consciously retrieve the solution word," at lags of 1.5 s (Bolte and Goschke, 2005 <!--ref:bolte2005speed-->). The annotation reports replication across the Bolte/Goschke, Topolinski/Strack and Volz lines, but these share co-authors (Bolte–Volz; Topolinski–Remmers), so independence is thinner than "multi-lab" implies, and the founding Bowers et al. (1990) data could not be anchored, so the claim rests on replications without the original. Topolinski and Strack (2009) <!--ref:topolinski2009architecture--> identify the mechanism — coherence raises fluency, fluency triggers brief positive affect, affect is read as coherence — and its vulnerability: "high fluency and positive affect independently and additively increased the probability that triads would be judged as coherent, irrespective of actual coherence," and participants could not correct even when aware. MEG shows OFC activation on coherent trials "began earlier in the OFC than in temporal object recognition areas" (Horr et al., 2014 <!--ref:horr2014feeling-->), supporting feeling-before-knowing, though at small N with same-lab convergence only (Grade C).

The one pre-registered study in the family, Remmers et al. (2020) <!--ref:remmers2020intuitive-->, found that "depressed patients and healthy controls did not differ in their general intuitive performance"; in that clinical sample (N = 35 + 35) fluency did not significantly drive coherence judgments in patients, whereas positive valence raised "coherent" responses in both groups. This is not read as evidence against the fluency route: a non-significant effect in a small clinical sample cannot outweigh eleven randomised experiments and the multi-lab fluency→coherence replications the annotation records. The defensible statement is that the affect route has pre-registered support while the fluency route has no pre-registered test and one small null. Koriat (1993) <!--ref:koriat1993how--> supplies the general principle: metacognitive feelings are heuristic inferences from accessible cues, valid to the extent the cues are. The accuracy prediction is testable: coherence and knowing feelings are accurate when the cue is causally tied to the truth of the judgment and systematically wrong when an irrelevant source supplies it. Zander et al. (2016) <!--ref:zander2016intuition--> keep this gradual process distinct from discontinuous insight.

#### Theme 4: "Knowing without knowing" dissociates in both directions; the feeling is a fallible inference, not a readout

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate · **Sources:** 4 (Levels V, VII) — koriat1993how, brown2003review, cowey2010blindsight, shanks1994characteristics

**Synthesis.** The exemplar phenomena pull apart. Blindsight is *knowledge without the feeling* (Cowey, 2010 <!--ref:cowey2010blindsight-->). Déjà vu is *the feeling without knowledge*: Brown (2003) <!--ref:brown2003review--> treats it as an illusion of recognition, reports that "about 60% of the population has experienced déjà vu," and notes it rises with stress and fatigue; that it carries no veridical content is the synthesis's gloss on his framing, not a quotation. The feeling-of-knowing sits between: FOK "challenge[s] the view that FOK is based on a direct, privileged access to an internal monitor" and is computed from partial accessibility, so it is accurate to the extent the accessible information is (Koriat, 1993 <!--ref:koriat1993how-->). A sense of knowing is therefore not itself evidence of knowledge; its diagnosticity is an empirical property of the situation — the formulation Theme 9 adopts. This partly answers the Devil's Advocate's strongest Checkpoint 1 counter-argument: the phenomenology is a real, lawful inference, not mere confabulation, but it is not a separate channel of knowledge.

#### Theme 5: Environment regularity and feedback are the proposed master boundary condition — a convergent framework with an ex ante operationalisation but no direct Level I–II test

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate (convergent framework; no direct Level I–II test) · **Sources:** 7 (Levels IV–VII; four traditions that cite one another and share authors) — kahneman2009conditions, hogarth2001educating, hogarth2015two, shanteau1992competence, tetlock2005expert, gigerenzer2011heuristic, lejarraga2021experimental

**Synthesis.** The heuristics-and-biases and naturalistic-decision-making traditions, writing jointly, agreed that intuitive expertise develops only where the environment is regular enough to be predictable and the person has had adequate opportunity to learn its regularities through feedback (Kahneman and Klein, 2009 <!--ref:kahneman2009conditions-->). Hogarth (2001) <!--ref:hogarth2001educating--> had named the contrast between kind environments (frequent, accurate feedback) and wicked ones, rooting intuition in "processes of tacit learning"; Hogarth et al. (2015) <!--ref:hogarth2015two--> formalised kindness: "kind learning environments involve close matches between the informational elements in the two settings and are a necessary condition for accurate inferences." Shanteau (1992) <!--ref:shanteau1992competence--> supplies the domain map — weather forecasters, livestock judges and chess players perform well; clinical psychologists, stockbrokers and parole officers poorly. Gigerenzer and Gaissmaier (2011) <!--ref:gigerenzer2011heuristic--> add the ecological-rationality version: whether a frugal, often unconscious rule performs well "is an empirical rather than an a priori issue" settled by environment structure. The traditions are convergent but not independent: Kahneman and Klein adopt Hogarth's distinction and rely on Shanteau's lists, and Hogarth's 2015 co-author Lejarraga is first author of the 2021 review.

**The circularity objection.** Kahneman and Klein's own annotation records that the environment-validity criterion is partly circular unless operationalised: classifying forecasting as wicked *because* forecasters failed, and chess as kind *because* players succeed, explains nothing. Two sources answer it ex ante. Hogarth et al. (2015) define kindness structurally — the match between the informational elements of the learning and prediction settings — which can be assessed before outcomes are known (selection effects, missing feedback, changed base rates). Lejarraga and Hertwig (2021) <!--ref:lejarraga2021experimental--> apply the same coding to 604 experiments and show that post-1974 research relied overwhelmingly on *described* rather than *experienced* tasks: "the focus on description at the expense of learning has profoundly shaped the influential view of the error-proneness of human cognition." Where a learning setting is present, statistical intuitions look far more competent. Part of the laboratory unreliability of gut feelings is thus an artefact of removing the learning setting the mechanism requires.

**What the field evidence does and does not show.** Tetlock (2005) <!--ref:tetlock2005expert--> is the canonical field demonstration of expert failure in a low-validity environment — over ~284 experts and ~20 years, probabilistic political forecasts barely beat extrapolation, and confidence was inversely related to accuracy, with integrative "foxes" outperforming "hedgehogs" — but the SQ2 annotation is explicit that these forecasts were deliberate, not strictly gut judgments, so Tetlock bounds *expert judgment broadly*, not intuition narrowly. Once that caveat is applied, SQ2 contains no Level I–III source that measured a *gut feeling specifically* in a wicked environment against the same feeling in a kind one. That is why the theme is Moderate: the framework is coherent, operationalisable and consistent with every result in SQ2, but it has not been tested as a prediction (Knowledge Gap 2).

#### Theme 6: Expertise moderates intuitive accuracy in the one direct test; the corpus has no Level I–III test of expertise-based gut feelings in a wicked environment

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate (one Grade C experiment plus reviews; the Level I sources bound a neighbouring construct) · **Sources:** 6 (Levels I, II, V) — dane2012trust, salas2010expertise, norman2017causes, norman2024dual, kuncel2013mechanical, grove2000clinical

**Synthesis.** Dane et al. (2012) <!--ref:dane2012trust--> give the only direct test: on non-decomposable tasks with objective answers, "the effectiveness of intuition relative to analysis is amplified at a high level of domain expertise," and novices gained nothing. Two cautions attach. The annotation says only "objective answers"; that these criteria were immediate (a kind environment) is the synthesis's inference. And because decision mode was instructed, an alternative reading — that analysis instructions *harmed* experts by shifting attention to verbalisable criteria, the mechanism of Wilson and Schooler (1991) <!--ref:wilson1991thinking--> — is not ruled out. Medicine parallels the expertise effect: "with increasing expertise (and knowledge), the likelihood of errors decreases" (Norman et al., 2017 <!--ref:norman2017causes-->), with the knowledge-based reframing of Norman et al. (2024) <!--ref:norman2024dual--> and the expertise-based intuition construct of Salas et al. (2010) <!--ref:salas2010expertise-->.

The two Level I meta-analyses do *not* bound expertise-based intuition; they bound deliberate holistic combination. Grove et al. (2000) <!--ref:grove2000clinical--> found mechanical techniques "about 10% more accurate than clinical predictions," with no rescue by judge experience, and Kuncel et al. (2013) <!--ref:kuncel2013mechanical--> found "consistent and substantial loss of validity when data were combined holistically—even by experts who are knowledgeable about the jobs and organizations in question." Both annotations state that clinical or holistic judgment here is unaided deliberate integration, not rapid gut feeling, and Grove's absolute effects are small. What they show is that *expert status does not protect deliberate holistic judgment in long-horizon prediction*. Whether expertise-based *gut feelings* degrade in the same environments is untested in the corpus; the Theme 5 framework predicts that they would, but that is a prediction, not a finding.

#### Theme 7: The unconscious-thought advantage did not survive independent replication; a memory-representation moderator remains

**Evidence Strength:** Strong (against the UTA); Emerging (gist/verbatim moderator) · **Sources:** 5 (Levels I, II, V) — dijksterhuis2006making, strick2011meta, nieuwenstein2015making, abadie2019evaluating, wilson1991thinking

**Synthesis.** Per the verification report's pairing rule, proponent and refuting evidence are read together. Complex purchases "were viewed more favorably when decisions had been made in the absence of attentive deliberation" (Dijksterhuis et al., 2006 <!--ref:dijksterhuis2006making-->), and the proponent meta-analysis pooled 92 studies to g = .224 (CI .145–.303) (Strick et al., 2011 <!--ref:strick2011meta-->). The independent, pre-specified N = 399 test found no advantage (58.2% vs 61.9%, n.s.), and its meta-analysis showed "previous reports of the UTA were confined to underpowered studies," trim-and-fill g = 0.018 (Nieuwenstein et al., 2015 <!--ref:nieuwenstein2015making-->). On authorship independence, pre-specification, sample size and bias correction, the null is better supported (verification grades C vs A). The sceptical result has its own limitation, recorded in its annotation: it tests only the multi-attribute choice paradigm with a normative, decomposable "best choice" criterion, which UTT proponents argue is not where unconscious thought should excel. That objection is answered within the corpus — Abadie and Waroquier (2019) <!--ref:abadie2019evaluating--> report that the null was later extended to other UTT paradigms — but it should be stated rather than assumed away.

What remains is more useful than the original claim. Abadie and Waroquier, independent authors with both positive and null studies, reframe the question as which memory representations are available: deliberation helps with verbatim detail, whereas "a distraction period is more useful when meaning-based gist representations of the alternatives are accessible," and immediate snap choice is never the best mode. Wilson and Schooler (1991) <!--ref:wilson1991thinking--> remain the strongest evidence that deliberation can actively harm — "analyzing reasons can focus people's attention on nonoptimal criteria" — but with small samples, an expert-agreement criterion on preferences and no located replication, "thinking too much" is rated Emerging. Net: a gut feeling does not become more accurate by withholding attention; it becomes more accurate when gist is the right representation for the task.

#### Theme 8: Rapid social perception carries real predictive validity, while deliberate holistic combination of data loses to formulas; the two literatures measure different constructs

**Evidence Strength:** Strong (both halves, Level I, each with stated limitations) · **Sources:** 4 (Levels I, III) — ambady1992thin, murphy2019predictive, grove2000clinical, kuncel2013mechanical

**Synthesis.** Thin slices under five minutes predict interpersonal outcomes at mean r = .39, and "studies using longer periods of behavioral observation did not yield greater predictive accuracy" (Ambady and Rosenthal, 1992 <!--ref:ambady1992thin-->). Three caveats travel with that figure: the anchor's provenance is indirect (two indexing records, no fetched page); several criteria (teacher effectiveness, therapist competence) are consensus ratings by other perceivers rather than ground truth, which the Phase 1 accuracy definition permits but the reader should know; and the 1992 bias-correction methods are dated. Coded 1-min slices show "some loss in predictive validity … but relatively little loss when Slices 1 and 2 were combined" (Murphy et al., 2019 <!--ref:murphy2019predictive-->), which bounds the information available to a perceiver, not the perceiver's judgment.

At the same evidence level, Grove et al. (2000) <!--ref:grove2000clinical--> and Kuncel et al. (2013) <!--ref:kuncel2013mechanical--> show unaided judgment losing to mechanical combination — with their own limitations: "clinical judgment" conflates intuition with deliberate reasoning; absolute effects are small (about 10%); many primary studies are older forensic and psychiatric predictions. The two halves are compatible once the construct table is applied: thin-slice accuracy is rapid pattern recognition over a high-regularity social cue domain; clinical-vs-mechanical is *deliberate holistic combination* against rules in long-horizon prediction. A plausible reading is that the gut can be a good cue detector and a poor cue combiner, and Grove's finding that clinicians did relatively worse with interview data fits it. This reconciliation is provisional: Betsch and Glöckner (2010), excluded only because no anchor could be retrieved, hold that intuition *is* effortless integration of large amounts of information, which would require reconciling the clinical-vs-mechanical result with a theory of intuitive integration rather than dissolving it by construct separation.

#### Theme 9: Confidence and the feeling of knowing are not reliably diagnostic of accuracy; their diagnosticity is itself a property of the learning environment

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate · **Sources:** 7 (Levels II–VII) — kahneman2009conditions, tetlock2005expert, koriat1993how, lufityanto2016measuring, topolinski2009architecture, brown2003review, dunn2010listening

**Synthesis.** "Subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgment accuracy" (Kahneman and Klein, 2009 <!--ref:kahneman2009conditions-->) is the right formulation: *not reliable*, not *uninformative*. The corpus shows both faces. Confidence fails in wicked settings — forecasters' confidence was inversely related to accuracy over 20 years (Tetlock, 2005 <!--ref:tetlock2005expert-->; deliberate forecasts, not gut judgments) — and feelings can be inflated by irrelevant cues: fluency and affect raise coherence judgments "irrespective of actual coherence" (Topolinski and Strack, 2009 <!--ref:topolinski2009architecture-->); a familiarity feeling experienced by about 60% of people has no veridical object (Brown, 2003 <!--ref:brown2003review-->); bodily signals strengthen whatever choice they point to (Dunn et al., 2010 <!--ref:dunn2010listening-->). But confidence also tracks accuracy where the cues are valid: the feeling of knowing is accurate to the extent that accessible partial information is itself correct, which explains both its validity and its illusions (Koriat, 1993 <!--ref:koriat1993how-->), and nonconscious emotional information that improved accuracy raised confidence with it (Lufityanto et al., 2016 <!--ref:lufityanto2016measuring-->). Integrating these, the confidence–accuracy relation is itself an output of the learning environment: calibrated where feedback was valid, uncalibrated or inverted where it was not. The practical consequence is that the strength of a gut feeling cannot *by itself* tell the judge which case applies; Themes 5 and 6 supply the external check.

#### Theme 10: The anomalous-cognition ("sixth sense") reading does not meet the pre-registered / replicated / robust standard where it has been tested; proponent effects are small, proponent-curated and non-pre-registered

**Evidence Strength:** Strong (against) for Bem's Experiments 1, 8 and 9; unreplicated in this corpus for Experiments 2–7; Emerging (against) for physiological presentiment, which is untested on the Phase 1 standard · **Sources:** 15 (Levels I, II, V, VII) — bem2011feeling, bem2016feeling, mossbridge2012predictive, mossbridge2014predicting, cardena2018experimental, wagenmakers2011why, rouder2011bayes, simmons2011false, ritchie2012failing, galak2012correcting, siller2015investigating, kekecs2023raising, walleczek2025metascientific, schwarzkopf2014should, reber2020searching

**What proponents claim.** Bem (2011) <!--ref:bem2011feeling--> reported mean d = 0.22 across nine experiments, N > 1,000, "all but one" significant one-tailed. The proponent meta-analysis pooled 90 experiments from 33 laboratories to g = 0.09; "the combined effect size for replications by independent investigators is 0.06, z = 4.16," with larger effects for implicit, fast-thinking protocols (Bem et al., 2016 <!--ref:bem2016feeling-->). The existence of that independent-investigator estimate is why the proponent evidence is described here as proponent-curated and non-pre-registered rather than author-dependent. For presentiment, Mossbridge et al. (2012) <!--ref:mossbridge2012predictive--> report ES = 0.21 (CI 0.15–0.27) across 26 reports and that "higher quality experiments produced a quantitatively larger effect size." Cardeña (2018) <!--ref:cardena2018experimental--> reads this as "cumulative support for the reality of psi, which cannot be readily explained away by the quality of the studies, fraud, selective reporting."

**What the critiques showed.** Default Bayesian reanalysis made the evidence "weak to nonexistent" (Wagenmakers et al., 2011 <!--ref:wagenmakers2011why-->); the corrected pooled Bayes factor, at most ≈40 for valenced non-erotic stimuli, is still "orders of magnitude lower than what is required to overcome appropriate skepticism of ESP" (Rouder and Morey, 2011 <!--ref:rouder2011bayes-->; a pooling of one paper's experiments, not a literature search). Simmons et al. (2011) <!--ref:simmons2011false--> gave the generative mechanism: undisclosed "flexibility in data collection, analysis, and reporting dramatically increases actual false-positive rates," enough to produce a d ≈ 0.2 effect with no true effect. For presentiment, Schwarzkopf (2014) <!--ref:schwarzkopf2014should--> — a Grade C opinion piece — argues flexible pre-stimulus windows and expectation bias suffice and demands pre-registered adversarial replication; the proponents themselves name "expectation bias and multiple analyses" as the two hardest validity threats and concede that links to conscious precognition have not produced clear results (Mossbridge et al., 2014 <!--ref:mossbridge2014predicting-->).

**What the pre-registered and mega-scale replications found.** Three pre-registered exact replications of Experiment 9: "combined n = 150; combined p = .83, one-tailed" (Ritchie et al., 2012 <!--ref:ritchie2012failing-->) — with power only ≈0.5–0.6 for d = 0.22, so not decisive alone. Seven replications of Experiments 8–9, N = 3,289: average d = 0.04, "no different from 0" (Galak et al., 2012 <!--ref:galak2012correcting-->; two of seven pre-registered, several online). A Registered Report on Experiment 1, co-designed by proponents and skeptics with born-open data and external audit: "49.89% successful guesses, while Bem reported 53.07%" (Kekecs et al., 2023 <!--ref:kekecs2023raising-->; cited with its 2023 correction notice listing protocol deviations from the Kennedy audit, which the notice states do not affect the null conclusion). N = 26,483 on Experiment 1 under the same protocol family: Study 1 null, Study 2 a confirmatory below-chance effect (49.65%, p = .013), Study 3 null at 50.07%, with the authors judging that "the second scenario" — a method-derived false positive — "appears more plausible" (Walleczek et al., 2025 <!--ref:walleczek2025metascientific-->). Study 2 was pre-registered and not analytically flexible; it is set aside because the same standard that rejects Bem's unreplicated positives rejects an unreplicated negative. For presentiment, Siller et al. (2015) <!--ref:siller2015investigating--> "could not find any evidence of presentiment but did find evidence of physiological correlates of expectation," modulated by randomisation scheme — but the design departs from classical presentiment protocols, so by the authors' own statement it cannot settle whether earlier positives were expectation confounds, and it is not pre-registered.

**The proponent replies, and the answers available in the corpus.** Three replies are recorded in the annotations. (1) *Experimenter scepticism moderates the effect* (raised against Galak 2012 and in Bem 2016). Kekecs 2023 answers it: the protocol was co-designed by a consensus panel including proponent Tressoldi, and the result was still null; more generally, an effect that appears only under proponent experimenters is unreplicable by construction and so cannot meet the Phase 1 standard. (2) *Psi-conducive conditions were absent* (raised against Kekecs 2023). This is an untested auxiliary hypothesis; it would need to be pre-specified and then shown to produce the effect, which no corpus source does. (3) *The default prior was unrealistic* (Bem, Utts & Johnson 2011, held back). Rouder and Morey's pooled Bayes factor already uses a more favourable analysis than Wagenmakers' and remains far below any plausible threshold, and the prior dispute is moot once pre-registered replications at N = 26,483 return chance.

**Adjudication on the Phase 1 standard.** Where the standard has been applied — Experiments 1, 8 and 9 — the claim fails all three criteria, and the null is supported by Grade A/B sources against Grade C proponent sources. Experiments 2–7 (priming, habituation, approach/avoidance) have no located replication in this corpus; Muhmenthaler et al. (2024; three Bem paradigms, >2,000 participants, null, not pre-registered) was held back under the cap and would widen coverage. Physiological presentiment has not been tested on the standard at all: its disconfirmation rests on one non-pre-registered Level II experiment that cannot by design settle the question, a Grade C opinion piece, and the proponents' own concession about confounds; Duggan and Tressoldi (2018; 27 new experiments, ES = 0.28, held back) is the item most likely to move this row, and the SQ3 stress test shows it is the label most sensitive to venue inclusion. The verdict is therefore scoped: the sixth-sense reading does not meet the standard where tested, and is untested rather than refuted for presentiment. Because parapsychology-field journals were excluded by design, this is a *mainstream-venue* verdict; it is also, through the multi-site mega-studies, the most culturally general finding in the report. The a priori arguments — "Claims made by parapsychologists cannot be true" (Reber and Alcock, 2020 <!--ref:reber2020searching-->) versus Cardeña's inventory — are Level VII with mirror-image stakes; the synthesis relies on neither.

#### Theme 11: Premonition experiences have candidate ordinary-cognition explanations that reuse the SQ1 mechanisms

**Evidence Strength:** Moderate · **Sources:** 5 (Levels II, IV, V) — wiseman2006belief, blackmore1985belief, siller2015investigating, with brown2003review and koriat1993how from SQ1

**Synthesis.** Why it *feels* like a sixth sense has candidate answers independent of whether psi exists. Believers made more probability errors and showed a "chance baseline shift" that "could underlie the illusion of control and the belief in psi" (Blackmore and Trościanko, 1985 <!--ref:blackmore1985belief-->; small N, mixed later replications). The misattribution hypothesis — probability misjudgement, fantasy proneness and correspondence-finding "increase the likelihood of … misattributing paranormal causation to experiences that have a normal explanation" — is reviewed by Wiseman and Watt (2006) <!--ref:wiseman2006belief-->, who note the evidence is inconsistent and correlational. Siller et al. (2015) <!--ref:siller2015investigating--> show in principle that the body anticipates the structure of the recent sequence, not the future. With Theme 3 (coherence feelings from fluency and affect), Theme 4 (déjà vu as recognition illusion; FOK as inference) and Theme 1 (pattern recognition over learned regularities), the ordinary account predicts the phenomenology the sixth-sense reading takes as evidence: a feeling arrives, its basis is not reportable, and hits are remembered. Moderate because the belief literature is small-sample and mixed, and mainstream research on spontaneous premonition reports and their base rates is thin.

### Knowledge Gaps

1. **No Level I synthesis for any SQ1 mechanism family.** No quantitative meta-analysis of expert-intuition accuracy, IGT/somatic-marker effects or coherence-intuition accuracy was *located* (PsycINFO/Scopus were unreachable, so this is a search ceiling, not a literature fact); the only Level I source touching SQ1 is a measurement meta-analysis (hickman2020relationship).
2. **No Level I–II test of the master boundary condition** in which environment kindness is manipulated or coded ex ante and intuitive accuracy is the outcome; the closest are lejarraga2021experimental and norman2024dual. Theme 5 is a framework, not a finding.
3. **No Level I–III evidence on expertise-based gut feelings in wicked environments.** The sources that show expert failure there (grove2000clinical, kuncel2013mechanical, tetlock2005expert) measured deliberate judgment. Primary NDM field evidence (fireground, nursing, chess, sport officiating) is absent; the forecasting-training literature that answers Tetlock 2005 is absent.
4. **Key pro-intuition primary results are unreplicated:** dane2012trust, lufityanto2016measuring, kandasamy2016interoceptive and wilson1991thinking; Lufityanto's 2022 follow-up is a preprint and was ineligible.
5. **The gist/verbatim moderator awaits independent large-N replication** (abadie2019evaluating).
6. **Physiological presentiment lacks a pre-registered adversarial test under the classic protocol**; Bem's Experiments 2–7 lack any located replication in this corpus.
7. **Spontaneous premonition experiences lack base-rate studies in mainstream venues.**
8. **Sample provenance.** All SQ1 empirical entries use Western, predominantly student, samples.
9. **Interoception measurement.** Whether heartbeat-counting-based findings hold on discrimination measures is unknown (hickman2020relationship).
10. **Three failed seeds would change the picture in known directions.** Betsch & Glöckner (2010) would challenge Theme 8's cue-detector/cue-combiner reconciliation; Reber (1989) would give Theme 1 primary implicit-learning evidence against the Shanks constraint; Bowers et al. (1990) would supply the founding Level II data to Theme 3. Net: stronger pro-intuition mechanism themes, harder reconciliation for clinical-vs-mechanical.

**What would change this verdict.** A pre-registered classic-protocol presentiment test; a pre-registered replication of Bem Experiments 2–7; a Level I–II ex ante test of environment kindness on gut-feeling accuracy; retrieval of the three failed seeds; and inclusion of the parapsychology-venue record (which could move presentiment from "against" to "contested" but is unlikely to move the Experiment 1 verdict against Kekecs + Walleczek).

### Theoretical Integration

**Dual-process theory.** The corpus supports a dual-process vocabulary but not the default-interventionist story in which fast intuition errs and slow analysis corrects. "Parallel-competitive formulations offer a more nuanced and realistic depiction" in which intuition and analysis run concurrently (Hodgkinson and Sadler-Smith, 2018 <!--ref:hodgkinson2018dynamics-->); the two modes are better understood as consequences of the kind of knowledge retrieved than as independent processors (Norman et al., 2024 <!--ref:norman2024dual-->); and the memory-representation account of the unconscious-thought literature (Abadie and Waroquier, 2019 <!--ref:abadie2019evaluating-->) points the same way. Together with Theme 7, these say that *what is in memory* — regularities learned under feedback; gist versus verbatim representations — predicts accuracy better than *which process ran*. Hu et al. (2025) <!--ref:hu2025intuition--> add a narrower, single-lab, model-based observation that reflective individuals relied more on intuition and performed better, where "intuition" is a drift-diffusion starting-point bias rather than a phenomenological gut feeling; it is consistent with the parallel-competitive view but does not speak to memory content. This answers the Checkpoint 1 counter-argument: the distinct functional category the corpus supports is not a separate knowledge channel but a distinct *output format*, a judgment delivered with a feeling and without its reasons, generated by ordinary learning and memory.

**Ecological rationality.** Gigerenzer and Gaissmaier (2011) <!--ref:gigerenzer2011heuristic-->, Hogarth et al. (2015) <!--ref:hogarth2015two--> and Lejarraga and Hertwig (2021) <!--ref:lejarraga2021experimental--> make one structural claim: the accuracy of a fast or intuitive judgment is a property of the fit between learning history and environment structure, "an empirical rather than an a priori issue." Kahneman and Klein (2009) <!--ref:kahneman2009conditions--> accept the same condition from the biases side. The kind/wicked frame is *consistent with* every SQ2 result — the clinical-vs-mechanical findings (wicked deliberate prediction), thin-slice validity (kind social perception), forecasting failure (wicked deliberate judgment), the expertise effect (plausibly kind classification) and the laboratory overestimate of error (description instead of experience) — but, as Theme 5 states, consistency with results classified after the fact is not a test. The sceptic's simpler story, that fast judgments are accurate exactly when the underlying knowledge is accurate and "kindness" is a description of that knowledge's provenance rather than an additional mechanism, is not distinguishable from the ecological account on this corpus. The ex ante operationalisation of Hogarth 2015 is what would let the two be distinguished.

**Somatic marker and interoceptive inference.** The somatic marker hypothesis (Bechara et al., 1997 <!--ref:bechara1997deciding-->) survives only in weak form: bodily signals are generated, perceived to varying degrees, and bias choice in the direction they point (Dunn et al., 2010 <!--ref:dunn2010listening-->), without evidence that they carry information the person lacks consciously (Maia and McClelland, 2004 <!--ref:maia2004reexamination-->; Dunn et al., 2006 <!--ref:dunn2006somatic-->). The contemporary predictive-processing or interoceptive-inference framing — bodily feelings as inferences about the causes of interoceptive signals — is not represented in the corpus. **[MATERIAL GAP]**: no annotated source supports a claim about interoceptive inference or predictive processing; the synthesis can only note that such a framing would be consistent with Koriat's feelings-as-inferences principle and the Dunn 2010 amplifier result, and recommend a targeted follow-up search. The compiler must not fill it from memory.

**How the frames fit.** Implicit learning supplies the content; fluency, affect and bodily state supply the *signal* that content has been matched; whether that signal was ever calibrated against truth depends on the learning history, which the kind/wicked frame describes. Across the three ordinary constructs the answer to "when is a gut feeling accurate?" is the same framework-level answer: when the feeling is the output of learning in a regular, feedback-rich setting and the present case belongs to that history. The anomalous-cognition construct alone denies this dependence, and where it has been tested on its own terms it has not met them.

### Plain-language answer

Does the evidence support a "sixth sense" — knowing about the future or hidden facts through a channel beyond ordinary perception, memory and learning? Where the claim has been put to the strictest test, no. The best-known claim (Bem's "feeling the future") produced small effects in studies that were not pre-registered and used flexible analyses; when three of its nine procedures were rerun under pre-registration by independent and mixed proponent–skeptic teams with tens of thousands of participants, the effect was at chance. The other six procedures have not been retested in the sources reviewed here. The related claim that the body reacts seconds before a random event rests on a proponent-run meta-analysis; it has an ordinary explanation shown in principle (bodily arousal tracks the pattern of what just happened) but not yet tested under the classic protocol with pre-registration, so the verdict there is "not supported and not yet properly tested," not "ruled out." All of this is the verdict of mainstream journals; parapsychology's own journals were outside scope.

What the evidence does support is that gut feelings are real, often accurate, and explicable without a sixth sense. They are the output of pattern learning that is largely not put into words — though how much of it is truly unconscious is still debated — delivered as a feeling of coherence, familiarity or bodily pull. The best-supported account says they are accurate when the person has had repeated experience with honest feedback in a situation with real regularities, and the present case is one of those; they are unreliable, often confidently so, when feedback is delayed, missing or misleading, when the feeling was produced by something irrelevant (fluency, mood), or when the task is one that a simple formula handles better than any holistic judgment. That account is a well-supported framework rather than a tested prediction: no study in this corpus manipulated the environment and measured gut-feeling accuracy as the outcome, and the clearest demonstrations of expert failure concern deliberate judgment rather than snap feelings. Finally, the strength of a gut feeling is only as trustworthy as the environment that trained it; it cannot by itself tell you which kind of environment you are in.
