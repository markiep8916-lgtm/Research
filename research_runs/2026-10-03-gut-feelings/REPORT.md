## Abstract

This report asks when gut-feeling judgments, which arrive with conviction but without reportable reasons, yield accurate knowledge, and how the empirical record treats the sixth-sense reading. A critical interpretive narrative synthesis covered 58 unique works (56 peer-reviewed articles and two university-press monographs), each verified for existence through Crossref (and OpenAlex for the 50 pre-sweep works) and graded on a seven-level hierarchy. Three ordinary constructs (implicit learning and expert pattern recognition, affective-somatic signals, and fluency-based metacognitive feelings) share one phenomenology but differ in mechanism. The best-supported account holds that gut feelings are accurate when they are the output of learning under valid feedback in a regular environment and the present case belongs to that history, a convergent framework (Moderate) with no direct Level I to II test. Somatic signals bias choice, but their nonconscious, causal role is not established (Emerging, contested); the unconscious-thought advantage did not survive independent replication (Strong against). Rapid social perception has predictive validity (Moderate; Level I with imperfect construct mapping), whereas deliberate holistic combination loses to formulas (Strong; a neighbouring construct). The anomalous-cognition reading fails the pre-registered, replicated, robust standard for Bem's Experiments 1, 8 and 9 (Strong against), is unreplicated in this corpus for Experiments 2 to 7, and is untested on that standard for presentiment (Emerging against). Confidence is not reliably diagnostic of accuracy; its diagnosticity depends on the learning environment.

**Keywords:** intuition; gut feeling; implicit learning; somatic marker hypothesis; processing fluency; kind and wicked learning environments; precognition replication


## 1. Introduction

A judgment arrives fully formed, carries a sense of rightness, and its basis cannot be reported: a gut feeling, knowing without knowing how, or, in its strongest folk reading, a sixth sense. The literatures that bear on it are fragmented. Dual-process theory treats intuition as a bridging construct between cognition and affect (Hodgkinson et al., 2008<!--ref:hodgkinson2008intuition--><!--anchor:quote:we%20clarify%20and%20distinguish%20intuition%20from%20related%20constructs%2C%20such%20as%20insight%2C%20and%20review%20a%20number%20of%20theoretical%20models%20that%20attempt%20to%20unify%20cognition%20and%20affect-->); social cognitive neuroscience proposes implicit learning as its substrate (Lieberman, 2000<!--ref:lieberman2000intuition--><!--anchor:quote:This%20review%20proposes%20that%20implicit%20learning%20processes%20are%20the%20cognitive%20substrate%20of%20social%20intuition.-->); the somatic marker tradition locates the feeling in the body (Bechara et al., 1997<!--ref:bechara1997deciding--><!--anchor:quote:Normals%20began%20to%20choose%20advantageously%20before%20they%20realized%20which%20strategy%20worked%20best-->); metacognition treats feelings of knowing as inferences, not readouts (Koriat, 1993<!--ref:koriat1993how--><!--anchor:quote:The%20results%20challenge%20the%20view%20that%20FOK%20is%20based%20on%20a%20direct%2C%20privileged%20access%20to%20an%20internal%20monitor.-->). A contested literature claims anticipations of future events with no ordinary channel (Bem, 2011<!--ref:bem2011feeling--><!--anchor:quote:The%20mean%20effect%20size%20%28d%29%20in%20psi%20performance%20across%20all%209%20experiments%20was%200.22%2C%20and%20all%20but%20one%20of%20the%20experiments%20yielded%20statistically%20significant%20results.-->). Because these literatures rarely speak to one another, evidence for one construct is routinely read as evidence for another; "sixth sense" itself carries a hypothesis about mechanism to be tested, not assumed.

The primary research question fixed in Phase 1 is:

> What mechanisms and boundary conditions explain when "gut feeling" judgments, judgments reached without conscious access to their basis, yield accurate knowledge?

Three sub-questions structure the synthesis: (1) the mechanisms by which gut-feeling judgments arise (implicit learning, pattern recognition, somatic signals, fluency); (2) the conditions under which they outperform, match, or underperform deliberate analysis (environment, expertise, feedback, task); and (3) how the empirical record treats the "sixth sense" reading, anomalous cognition, relative to ordinary explanations.


## 2. Literature review and theoretical framework

### 2.1 Separating the constructs

Table 1 separates the constructs covered by "intuition", as the Phase 1 devil's advocate required; its sixth row names a neighbouring construct that several Level I sources measure but that is not a gut feeling.

**Table 1. Construct definitions**

|---|---|---|---|
| Implicit learning / expert pattern recognition | Rapid, nonconscious, holistic association over regularities acquired through experience (Lieberman, 2000<!--ref:lieberman2000intuition--><!--anchor:quote:This%20review%20proposes%20that%20implicit%20learning%20processes%20are%20the%20cognitive%20substrate%20of%20social%20intuition.-->; Dane & Pratt, 2007<!--ref:dane2007exploring--><!--anchor:quote:defining%20intuitions%20as%20affectively%20charged%20judgments%20that%20arise%20through%20rapid%2C%20nonconscious%2C%20and%20holistic%20associations-->) | "I just know this is right"; basis not reportable | Accurate when the environment had learnable regularities and feedback was valid, a kind environment being "a necessary condition for accurate inferences" (Hogarth et al., 2015<!--ref:hogarth2015two--><!--anchor:quote:Kind%20learning%20environments%20involve%20close%20matches%20between%20the%20informational%20elements%20in%20the%20two%20settings%20and%20are%20a%20necessary%20condition%20for%20accurate%20inferences.-->); otherwise confidently wrong, because "Subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgment accuracy" (Kahneman & Klein, 2009<!--ref:kahneman2009conditions--><!--anchor:quote:Subjective%20experience%20is%20not%20a%20reliable%20indicator%20of%20judgment%20accuracy.-->) |

| Neighbouring construct (not a gut feeling): deliberate holistic expert judgment | Unaided human integration of case data, or forecasting over months to years; includes intuition but is not reducible to it (Grove et al., 2000<!--ref:grove2000clinical--><!--anchor:quote:On%20average%2C%20mechanical-prediction%20techniques%20were%20about%2010%25%20more%20accurate%20than%20clinical%20predictions.-->; Kuncel et al., 2013<!--ref:kuncel2013mechanical--><!--anchor:quote:There%20was%20consistent%20and%20substantial%20loss%20of%20validity%20when%20data%20were%20combined%20holistically%E2%80%94even%20by%20experts%20who%20are%20knowledgeable%20about%20the%20jobs%20and%20organizations%20in%20question-->; Tetlock, 2005<!--ref:tetlock2005expert--><!--anchor:quote:is%20better%20able%20to%20improvise%20in%20response%20to%20changing%20events%E2%80%94is%20more%20successful%20in%20predicting%20the%20future%20than%20the%20hedgehog-->) | Considered professional judgment | Bounds human judgment broadly; used only to bound the neighbouring construct, never as direct evidence about gut feelings |

The first three share a phenomenology but differ in mechanism and accuracy conditions; only the fifth predicts accuracy independent of prior learning, so its evidentiary standard differs in kind.

### 2.2 Three theoretical frames

**Dual-process theory** supplies most of the corpus's vocabulary; Hodgkinson et al. (2008<!--ref:hodgkinson2008intuition--><!--anchor:quote:we%20clarify%20and%20distinguish%20intuition%20from%20related%20constructs%2C%20such%20as%20insight%2C%20and%20review%20a%20number%20of%20theoretical%20models%20that%20attempt%20to%20unify%20cognition%20and%20affect-->) place intuition inside it while distinguishing it from insight. The default-interventionist version (fast intuition errs, slow analysis corrects) is not the only one: parallel-competitive formulations run the two concurrently (Hodgkinson & Sadler-Smith, 2018†<!--ref:hodgkinson2018dynamics--><!--anchor:quote:parallel-competitive%20formulations%20offer%20a%20more%20nuanced%20and%20realistic%20depiction%20of%20organizational%20decision%20makers%20as%20thinking%20and%20feeling%20beings-->; † marks recency-sweep entries, Section 3.5), and in clinical reasoning the two modes reflect the knowledge retrieved rather than separate processors (Norman et al., 2024†<!--ref:norman2024dual--><!--anchor:quote:they%20arise%20from%20lack%20of%20access%20to%20the%20appropriate%20knowledge%2C%20not%20from%20errors%20of%20processing-->).

**Ecological rationality and learning environments** treat accuracy as the fit between learning history and environment: how well a heuristic performs is an empirical, not an a priori, matter (Gigerenzer & Gaissmaier, 2011<!--ref:gigerenzer2011heuristic--><!--anchor:quote:it%20is%20an%20empirical%20rather%20than%20an%20a%20priori%20issue%20how%20well%20cognitive%20heuristics%20function%20in%20an%20uncertain%20world-->). Hogarth (2001<!--ref:hogarth2001educating--><!--anchor:quote:intuition%20is%20a%20normal%20and%20important%20component%20of%20thought%20that%20has%20its%20roots%20in%20processes%20of%20tacit%20learning-->) rooted intuition in "processes of tacit learning" and contrasted kind environments, with frequent accurate feedback, against wicked ones, which breed confident but invalid intuitions; Hogarth et al. (2015<!--ref:hogarth2015two--><!--anchor:quote:Kind%20learning%20environments%20involve%20close%20matches%20between%20the%20informational%20elements%20in%20the%20two%20settings%20and%20are%20a%20necessary%20condition%20for%20accurate%20inferences.-->) formalised kindness as a match between learning and prediction settings; Shanteau (1992<!--ref:shanteau1992competence--><!--anchor:quote:the%20cognitive%20skills%20necessary%20to%20make%20tough%20decisions%2C%20the%20ability%20to%20use%20appropriate%20decision%20strategies%2C%20and%20a%20task%20with%20suitable%20characteristics-->) maps where expert competence is found.

**Somatic markers and interoception** offer the most literal model: bodily states generated by past outcomes bias present choice before, or without, explicit knowledge (Bechara et al., 1997<!--ref:bechara1997deciding--><!--anchor:quote:Normals%20began%20to%20choose%20advantageously%20before%20they%20realized%20which%20strategy%20worked%20best-->), challenged on its own paradigm (Maia & McClelland, 2004<!--ref:maia2004reexamination--><!--anchor:quote:participants%20report%20knowledge%20of%20the%20advantageous%20strategy%20more%20reliably%20than%20they%20behave%20advantageously-->; Dunn et al., 2006<!--ref:dunn2006somatic--><!--anchor:quote:their%20interpretation%20is%20undermined%20by%20the%20cognitive%20penetrability%20of%20the%20reward/punishment%20schedule%2C%20ambiguity%20surrounding%20interpretation%20of%20the%20psychophysiological%20data%2C%20and%20a%20shortage%20of%20causal%20evidence-->) and refined into an amplifier account (Dunn et al., 2010<!--ref:dunn2010listening--><!--anchor:quote:increasing%20interoception%20ability%20either%20helped%20or%20hindered%20adaptive%20intuitive%20decision%20making%2C%20depending%20on%20whether%20the%20anticipatory%20bodily%20signals%20generated%20favored%20advantageous%20or%20disadvantageous%20choices-->; Theme 2). The interoceptive-inference framing is not represented in the corpus (material gap; Section 5.1).


### 2.3 Research gap

Reviews exist within each sub-field; a synthesis that keeps the constructs apart, reads replication failures alongside original claims, and holds the sixth-sense reading to the same standard is less common, which is why Phase 1 rated the question feasible but only moderately novel.


## 3. Methodology

### 3.1 Design and scope assumptions

The study is a critical interpretive narrative synthesis of reviews, meta-analyses and key primary studies, organised by sub-question under a pragmatist paradigm, with contradictions resolved or recorded by explicit comparison of evidence quality. Because the Phase 1 user confirmation could not be obtained, scope was fixed by assumption (full list in the Phase 1 scoping record). Population: human adults. Time frame: 1990 to 2026 plus justified seminal earlier works. Disciplines: psychology, cognitive neuroscience, judgment and decision making, management and naturalistic decision making, with parapsychology reached only through replications, meta-analyses and critiques in mainstream journals. Definitions: "gut feeling" as a judgment whose basis is not consciously reportable; "accuracy" against an external criterion (correct answer, later outcome, or expert consensus); "sixth sense" as a hypothesis, not a premise. Out of scope: animal cognition, religious framings, self-help literature, and parapsychology beyond the replication record.

### 3.3 Inclusion and exclusion

Included sources were English-language peer-reviewed journal articles (or, for sub-question 2, university-press monographs with a catalogue record) on human adults. They had to address a mechanism or boundary condition of gut-feeling judgments, the comparative accuracy of intuitive and deliberate judgment, or, for sub-question 3, anomalous anticipation, its critique or replication, or ordinary explanations of psi experiences. Each had to pass existence verification and yield a verbatim quote anchor of at most 25 words. Excluded were child and animal samples, non-peer-reviewed items, parapsychology-field journals, unverifiable sources, and verified sources with no obtainable anchor: Reber (1989), Bowers et al. (1990) and Betsch and Glöckner (2010) are named as excluded, not cited, and Section 5.3 states what each would change. Works named without a reference entry are listed in Appendix D.


### 3.4 Evidence grading and strength labels

Each source was placed on a seven-level hierarchy: Level I, meta-analyses and systematic reviews; II, randomised experiments and pre-registered replications; III, controlled non-randomised designs; IV, observational and pre-existing-group comparisons; V, narrative or critical reviews; VI, single descriptive studies and case reports (none was assigned); VII, expert opinion and theory. A credibility grade (A to D) describes fitness to carry the specific claim an entry supports; level corrections are in Appendix B. Proponent and refuting evidence are read together. Strength labels take three values. Strong: at least one Level I or II source with independent replication or a pre-registered large-N test, converging with other levels. Moderate: converging Level II to V sources without independent replication of the key result, a Level I result with imperfect construct mapping, or a convergent Level IV to VII framework with no direct Level I or II test. Emerging: single-lab, unreplicated, measurement-contested or untested results, or Level VII alone.

### 3.5 Deterministic citation-existence verification

Every DOI was resolved against Crossref with a title match; all 58 unique works resolved. The 50 pre-sweep works were re-queried in OpenAlex and Crossref: 0 retractions, 0 expressions of concern, and one correction notice, attached to Kekecs et al. (2023<!--ref:kekecs2023raising--><!--anchor:quote:We%20found%2049.89%25%20successful%20guesses%2C%20while%20Bem%20reported%2053.07%25%20success%20rate%2C%20with%20the%20chance%20level%20being%2050%25.-->), which (https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.231080) lists protocol deviations identified in an external audit and states that the null conclusion is unaffected; this report names it here, in Theme 10 and in Section 5.3, and lists it in Appendix D. Conflicts of interest, proponent and critic alike, are named with institutions in Appendix B. The Phase 2 bibliographic annotations (source notes) are pipeline working documents, not sources a reader can consult; where this report relies on a claim found only there, it says so.

### 3.7 Limitations by design

This is not a PRISMA systematic review; sampling was purposive, PsycINFO, Scopus and Web of Science were not accessible, and absence of a source from the corpus is a search ceiling rather than a literature fact. The corpus was seed-driven by the field's best-known controversies, which favours claim-then-refutation narratives and under-samples the less glamorous field literature; sceptical anchors are paired with the limitations recorded in their annotations, but critiques not recorded there could not be added. Every quote anchor is abstract-level (publisher-description-level for the two monographs; indirectly indexed for Ambady & Rosenthal, 1992), so no page-level locators are claimed and quotation marks are confined to verified anchors. Accuracy against an external criterion admits expert-consensus criteria and excludes preference judgments; a phenomenological tradition would dispute this pragmatist choice. Parapsychology-field journals were excluded by design, so the sub-question 3 verdict is a mainstream-venue verdict. The tension inventory scanned 29 of 1,653 possible source pairs, so absence of a pair from Table 3 is not evidence of compatibility.

### 4.1 Sub-question 1: Mechanisms

#### Theme 1. Implicit learning and knowledge-based pattern recognition

**Evidence strength: Moderate** (nine sources, Levels V to VII; several from the same programmes).

Reviews and position papers converge, partly within programmes, on most accurate gut feelings being learned regularities retrieved without reportable reasoning. Lieberman (2000<!--ref:lieberman2000intuition--><!--anchor:quote:This%20review%20proposes%20that%20implicit%20learning%20processes%20are%20the%20cognitive%20substrate%20of%20social%20intuition.-->) proposes implicit learning as the substrate of social intuition; Dane and Pratt (2007<!--ref:dane2007exploring--><!--anchor:quote:defining%20intuitions%20as%20affectively%20charged%20judgments%20that%20arise%20through%20rapid%2C%20nonconscious%2C%20and%20holistic%20associations-->) define intuitions as affectively charged, rapid, nonconscious, holistic associations; Salas et al. (2010<!--ref:salas2010expertise--><!--anchor:quote:researchers%20have%20found%20that%20intuition%20plays%20a%20critical%20role%20in%20expert%20decision%20making-->) find that "intuition plays a critical role in expert decision making". In clinical reasoning, "with increasing expertise (and knowledge), the likelihood of errors decreases" (Norman et al., 2017<!--ref:norman2017causes--><!--anchor:quote:with%20increasing%20expertise%20%28and%20knowledge%29%2C%20the%20likelihood%20of%20errors%20decreases-->), slowing down does not help (source notes, not the anchor), and errors "arise from lack of access to the appropriate knowledge, not from errors of processing" (Norman et al., 2024†<!--ref:norman2024dual--><!--anchor:quote:they%20arise%20from%20lack%20of%20access%20to%20the%20appropriate%20knowledge%2C%20not%20from%20errors%20of%20processing-->). The mechanism predicts accuracy when regularities exist, were learned with valid feedback, and the present case belongs to the learned distribution, the conditions on which Kahneman and Klein (2009<!--ref:kahneman2009conditions--><!--anchor:quote:Subjective%20experience%20is%20not%20a%20reliable%20indicator%20of%20judgment%20accuracy.-->) converged while warning that "Subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgment accuracy"; Themes 1 and 5 describe one account.

The constraint is Shanks and St. John (1994<!--ref:shanks1994characteristics--><!--anchor:quote:We%20conclude%20that%20unconscious%20learning%20has%20not%20been%20satisfactorily%20established%20in%20any%20of%20these%20areas.-->): unconscious learning "has not been satisfactorily established" by their information and sensitivity criteria, which still apply even though the conclusion is no longer consensus. Blindsight is the cleanest existence proof of knowledge without awareness, since patients "detect, localise and even discriminate visual stimuli that they deny seeing" (Cowey, 2010<!--ref:cowey2010blindsight--><!--anchor:quote:detect%2C%20localise%20and%20even%20discriminate%20visual%20stimuli%20that%20they%20deny%20seeing-->), though artefacts are catalogued. Moderate because the theme rests on reviews: no primary implicit-learning study (Reber, 1989, excluded for lack of an anchor) and no Level I synthesis of expert-intuition accuracy.


#### Theme 2. Somatic and interoceptive signals

**Evidence strength: Emerging (contested)** (seven sources, Levels I to V).

The somatic marker hypothesis holds that "Normals began to choose advantageously before they realized which strategy worked best" (Bechara et al., 1997<!--ref:bechara1997deciding--><!--anchor:quote:Normals%20began%20to%20choose%20advantageously%20before%20they%20realized%20which%20strategy%20worked%20best-->; 10 controls, 6 patients). The "before knowing" step did not survive scrutiny: with quantitative probes, "participants report knowledge of the advantageous strategy more reliably than they behave advantageously" (Maia & McClelland, 2004<!--ref:maia2004reexamination--><!--anchor:quote:participants%20report%20knowledge%20of%20the%20advantageous%20strategy%20more%20reliably%20than%20they%20behave%20advantageously-->; about 20 participants, no psychophysiology), and Dunn et al. (2006<!--ref:dunn2006somatic--><!--anchor:quote:their%20interpretation%20is%20undermined%20by%20the%20cognitive%20penetrability%20of%20the%20reward/punishment%20schedule%2C%20ambiguity%20surrounding%20interpretation%20of%20the%20psychophysiological%20data%2C%20and%20a%20shortage%20of%20causal%20evidence-->) found the evidence undermined by cognitive penetrability, ambiguous psychophysiology and absent causal evidence. The resolution rests not on N but on probe sensitivity, on later sensitive-probe replications (source notes, not independently verified here) and on the absence of any causal test; the probe-reactivity reply (Bechara et al., 2005; Appendix D) would at most restore uncertainty about timing. Bodily co-variation with choice stands.

Interoceptive ability "either helped or hindered adaptive intuitive decision making", depending on which choice the signal favoured (Dunn et al., 2010<!--ref:dunn2010listening--><!--anchor:quote:increasing%20interoception%20ability%20either%20helped%20or%20hindered%20adaptive%20intuitive%20decision%20making%2C%20depending%20on%20whether%20the%20anticipatory%20bodily%20signals%20generated%20favored%20advantageous%20or%20disadvantageous%20choices-->): it amplifies without validating. Kandasamy et al. (2016<!--ref:kandasamy2016interoceptive--><!--anchor:quote:the%20interoceptive%20ability%20of%20traders%20predicted%20their%20relative%20profitability-->) supply the one field result, traders' interoceptive ability predicting profitability, but at N = 18, cross-sectional and unreplicated, it is an illustration only (Grade C). Lufityanto et al. (2016<!--ref:lufityanto2016measuring--><!--anchor:quote:nonconscious%20emotional%20information%20can%20boost%20accuracy%20and%20confidence%20in%20a%20concurrent%20emotion-free%20decision%20task%2C%20while%20also%20speeding%20up%20response%20times-->) show that nonconscious emotional information boosts accuracy and confidence, but only when valence was predictively paired with the correct answer (this report's reading), itself a learned contingency; no independent replication was located. The two cardiac interoceptive-accuracy tasks show only "a small relationship" across 22 studies (Hickman et al., 2020†<!--ref:hickman2020relationship--><!--anchor:quote:Pooled%20findings%20from%2022%20studies%20revealed%20a%20small%20relationship%20between%20accuracy%20scores%20on%20the%20measures.-->), so claims built on one task cannot be assumed on the other.


#### Theme 3. Coherence intuitions: fast, above chance, and vulnerable to irrelevant cues

**Evidence strength: Moderate** (Level II, replicated across three interlinked lab lines; no pre-registered test of the above-chance effect itself; six sources, Levels II to VII).

Participants "discriminated coherent and incoherent triads reliably better than chance, even when they did not consciously retrieve the solution word" (Bolte & Goschke, 2005<!--ref:bolte2005speed--><!--anchor:quote:participants%20discriminated%20coherent%20and%20incoherent%20triads%20reliably%20better%20than%20chance%2C%20even%20when%20they%20did%20not%20consciously%20retrieve%20the%20solution%20word-->). Replication across three lab lines is recorded, but they share co-authors, and the founding Bowers et al. (1990) data could not be anchored. Across eleven experiments Topolinski and Strack (2009<!--ref:topolinski2009architecture--><!--anchor:quote:high%20fluency%20and%20positive%20affect%20independently%20and%20additively%20increased%20the%20probability%20that%20triads%20would%20be%20judged%20as%20coherent%2C%20irrespective%20of%20actual%20coherence-->) identify the mechanism and its vulnerability: coherence raises fluency, fluency triggers brief positive affect, and affect is read as coherence, so both raised coherence judgments "irrespective of actual coherence", uncorrected even when participants were aware. Orbitofrontal activation on coherent trials begins earlier than in temporal object-recognition areas (Horr et al., 2014<!--ref:horr2014feeling--><!--anchor:quote:this%20increase%20in%20activation%20began%20earlier%20in%20the%20OFC%20than%20in%20temporal%20object%20recognition%20areas-->), consistent with feeling before knowing, though at small N with same-lab convergence only (Grade C).

The one pre-registered study found that "depressed patients and healthy controls did not differ in their general intuitive performance" (Remmers et al., 2020†<!--ref:remmers2020intuitive--><!--anchor:quote:depressed%20patients%20and%20healthy%20controls%20did%20not%20differ%20in%20their%20general%20intuitive%20performance-->; 35 patients, 35 controls); fluency did not significantly drive coherence judgments in patients, whereas positive valence did in both groups. A small clinical null cannot outweigh eleven randomised experiments, so the affect route has pre-registered support while the fluency route has none. Koriat (1993<!--ref:koriat1993how--><!--anchor:quote:The%20results%20challenge%20the%20view%20that%20FOK%20is%20based%20on%20a%20direct%2C%20privileged%20access%20to%20an%20internal%20monitor.-->) supplies the principle that metacognitive feelings are heuristic inferences from accessible cues (Theme 4); Zander et al. (2016<!--ref:zander2016intuition--><!--anchor:quote:intuition%20has%20been%20understood%20as%20an%20experience-based%20and%20gradual%20process%2C%20whereas%20insight%20is%20regarded%20as%20a%20genuinely%20discontinuous%20phenomenon-->) keep this gradual process distinct from discontinuous insight.


#### Theme 4. Knowing and the feeling of knowing dissociate in both directions

**Evidence strength: Moderate** (four sources, Levels V and VII).

Blindsight is knowledge without the feeling (Cowey, 2010<!--ref:cowey2010blindsight--><!--anchor:quote:detect%2C%20localise%20and%20even%20discriminate%20visual%20stimuli%20that%20they%20deny%20seeing-->); déjà vu is the feeling without knowledge, experienced by "About 60% of the population" (Brown, 2003<!--ref:brown2003review--><!--anchor:quote:About%2060%25%20of%20the%20population%20has%20experienced%20d%C3%A9j%C3%A0%20vu%2C%20and%20its%20frequency%20decreases%20with%20age.-->), and that it carries no veridical content is this report's gloss. The feeling of knowing sits between: the results "challenge the view that FOK is based on a direct, privileged access to an internal monitor", so it is accurate to the extent the accessible information is (Koriat, 1993<!--ref:koriat1993how--><!--anchor:quote:The%20results%20challenge%20the%20view%20that%20FOK%20is%20based%20on%20a%20direct%2C%20privileged%20access%20to%20an%20internal%20monitor.-->). A sense of knowing is therefore not itself evidence of knowledge (Theme 9). This partly answers the Checkpoint 1 counter-argument that there is nothing here beyond fast cognition with confabulated introspection: the phenomenology is a lawful inference, not a separate channel, and its unconscious status remains constrained by the Shanks criteria (Shanks & St. John, 1994<!--ref:shanks1994characteristics--><!--anchor:quote:We%20conclude%20that%20unconscious%20learning%20has%20not%20been%20satisfactorily%20established%20in%20any%20of%20these%20areas.-->).

### 4.2 Sub-question 2: Boundary conditions


#### Theme 5. Environment regularity and feedback as the master boundary condition

**Evidence strength: Moderate (convergent framework; no direct Level I to II test)** (seven sources, Levels IV to VII; four traditions that cite one another and share authors).

The heuristics-and-biases and naturalistic-decision-making traditions converged jointly on the conditions for intuitive expertise: a predictable environment and adequate opportunity to learn its regularities through feedback. The anchor verified for Kahneman and Klein (2009<!--ref:kahneman2009conditions--><!--anchor:quote:Subjective%20experience%20is%20not%20a%20reliable%20indicator%20of%20judgment%20accuracy.-->) carries the companion conclusion that "Subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgment accuracy"; the conditions themselves are anchored in Hogarth et al. (2015<!--ref:hogarth2015two--><!--anchor:quote:Kind%20learning%20environments%20involve%20close%20matches%20between%20the%20informational%20elements%20in%20the%20two%20settings%20and%20are%20a%20necessary%20condition%20for%20accurate%20inferences.-->): kind environments "are a necessary condition for accurate inferences". With Hogarth (2001<!--ref:hogarth2001educating--><!--anchor:quote:intuition%20is%20a%20normal%20and%20important%20component%20of%20thought%20that%20has%20its%20roots%20in%20processes%20of%20tacit%20learning-->), Shanteau (1992<!--ref:shanteau1992competence--><!--anchor:quote:the%20cognitive%20skills%20necessary%20to%20make%20tough%20decisions%2C%20the%20ability%20to%20use%20appropriate%20decision%20strategies%2C%20and%20a%20task%20with%20suitable%20characteristics-->) and Gigerenzer and Gaissmaier (2011<!--ref:gigerenzer2011heuristic--><!--anchor:quote:it%20is%20an%20empirical%20rather%20than%20an%20a%20priori%20issue%20how%20well%20cognitive%20heuristics%20function%20in%20an%20uncertain%20world-->), these traditions are convergent but not independent: Kahneman and Klein adopt Hogarth's distinction and Shanteau's lists, and Lejarraga co-authored Hogarth et al. (2015).

The environment-validity criterion is partly circular unless operationalised, an objection this report raises and that Kahneman and Klein do not address in the anchored text, since classifying forecasting as wicked because forecasters failed explains nothing; two sources answer it ex ante. Hogarth et al. (2015<!--ref:hogarth2015two--><!--anchor:quote:Kind%20learning%20environments%20involve%20close%20matches%20between%20the%20informational%20elements%20in%20the%20two%20settings%20and%20are%20a%20necessary%20condition%20for%20accurate%20inferences.-->) define kindness structurally, as "close matches between the informational elements in the two settings", assessable before outcomes are known. Lejarraga and Hertwig (2021†<!--ref:lejarraga2021experimental--><!--anchor:quote:the%20focus%20on%20description%20at%20the%20expense%20of%20learning%20has%20profoundly%20shaped%20the%20influential%20view%20of%20the%20error-proneness%20of%20human%20cognition-->) code 604 experiments and show that research relied overwhelmingly on described rather than experienced tasks, so that "the focus on description at the expense of learning has profoundly shaped the influential view of the error-proneness of human cognition".

Tetlock (2005<!--ref:tetlock2005expert--><!--anchor:quote:is%20better%20able%20to%20improvise%20in%20response%20to%20changing%20events%E2%80%94is%20more%20successful%20in%20predicting%20the%20future%20than%20the%20hedgehog-->) is the canonical demonstration of expert failure in a low-validity environment: over about 284 experts and 20 years, forecasts barely beat extrapolation and confidence was inversely related to accuracy, while the fox "is more successful in predicting the future than the hedgehog" (figures from the source notes). Those forecasts were deliberate, so Tetlock bounds expert judgment broadly, not intuition narrowly. Sub-question 2 therefore contains no Level I to III source that measured a gut feeling in a wicked environment against the same feeling in a kind one; the framework is consistent with every result but untested as a prediction.


#### Theme 6. Expertise as a moderator in the one direct test

**Evidence strength: Moderate** (one Grade C experiment plus reviews; the Level I sources bound a neighbouring construct; seven sources, Levels I, II and V).

Dane et al. (2012<!--ref:dane2012trust--><!--anchor:quote:the%20effectiveness%20of%20intuition%20relative%20to%20analysis%20is%20amplified%20at%20a%20high%20level%20of%20domain%20expertise-->) give the only direct test: "the effectiveness of intuition relative to analysis is amplified at a high level of domain expertise", and novices gained nothing. Two cautions attach: that the tasks' objective, immediate criteria made the environment kind is this report's inference from the source notes, not the anchor; and because decision mode was instructed, analysis instructions may have harmed experts by shifting attention to verbalisable criteria (Wilson & Schooler, 1991<!--ref:wilson1991thinking--><!--anchor:quote:Analyzing%20reasons%20can%20focus%20people%27s%20attention%20on%20nonoptimal%20criteria%2C%20causing%20them%20to%20base%20their%20subsequent%20choices%20on%20these%20criteria.-->). Medicine parallels the expertise effect (Norman et al., 2017<!--ref:norman2017causes--><!--anchor:quote:with%20increasing%20expertise%20%28and%20knowledge%29%2C%20the%20likelihood%20of%20errors%20decreases-->; Norman et al., 2024†<!--ref:norman2024dual--><!--anchor:quote:they%20arise%20from%20lack%20of%20access%20to%20the%20appropriate%20knowledge%2C%20not%20from%20errors%20of%20processing-->; Salas et al., 2010<!--ref:salas2010expertise--><!--anchor:quote:researchers%20have%20found%20that%20intuition%20plays%20a%20critical%20role%20in%20expert%20decision%20making-->). The two Level I meta-analyses, Grove et al. (2000<!--ref:grove2000clinical--><!--anchor:quote:On%20average%2C%20mechanical-prediction%20techniques%20were%20about%2010%25%20more%20accurate%20than%20clinical%20predictions.-->) and Kuncel et al. (2013<!--ref:kuncel2013mechanical--><!--anchor:quote:There%20was%20consistent%20and%20substantial%20loss%20of%20validity%20when%20data%20were%20combined%20holistically%E2%80%94even%20by%20experts%20who%20are%20knowledgeable%20about%20the%20jobs%20and%20organizations%20in%20question-->), bound deliberate holistic combination, not expertise-based intuition (Theme 8); whether expertise-based gut feelings degrade in the same environments is untested in the corpus, and the Theme 5 framework's prediction that they would is a prediction, not a finding.


#### Theme 7. The unconscious-thought advantage and its replication record

**Evidence strength: Strong (against the unconscious-thought advantage); Emerging (gist/verbatim moderator)** (five sources, Levels I, II and V).

Proponent and refuting evidence are read together (Section 3.4). Complex purchases "were viewed more favorably when decisions had been made in the absence of attentive deliberation" (Dijksterhuis et al., 2006<!--ref:dijksterhuis2006making--><!--anchor:quote:purchases%20of%20complex%20products%20were%20viewed%20more%20favorably%20when%20decisions%20had%20been%20made%20in%20the%20absence%20of%20attentive%20deliberation-->); the proponent meta-analysis pooled 92 studies to g = .224 (Strick et al., 2011<!--ref:strick2011meta--><!--anchor:quote:Across%20a%20total%20of%2092%20studies%2C%20the%20overall%20aggregated%20effect%20size%20was%20g%20%3D%20.224%2C%20with%20a%2095%25%20confidence%20interval%20from%20.145%20to%20.303.-->). The independent, pre-specified N = 399 test found no advantage, and its meta-analysis showed that "previous reports of the UTA were confined to underpowered studies", trim-and-fill g = 0.018 (Nieuwenstein et al., 2015<!--ref:nieuwenstein2015making--><!--anchor:quote:the%20large-scale%20replication%20study%20yielded%20no%20evidence%20for%20the%20UTA%2C%20and%20the%20meta-analysis%20showed%20that%20previous%20reports%20of%20the%20UTA%20were%20confined%20to%20underpowered%20studies-->); on independence, pre-specification, sample size and bias correction the null is better supported (grades C versus A). Its recorded limitation is that it tests only multi-attribute choice with a normative criterion, which proponents argue is not where unconscious thought should excel; Abadie and Waroquier (2019†<!--ref:abadie2019evaluating--><!--anchor:quote:a%20distraction%20period%20is%20more%20useful%20when%20meaning-based%20gist%20representations%20of%20the%20alternatives%20are%20accessible-->) report the null extended to other paradigms (source notes; their anchor supports only the moderator below).

What remains is a memory-representation moderator: deliberation helps with verbatim detail, whereas "a distraction period is more useful when meaning-based gist representations of the alternatives are accessible", and a snap choice is never the best mode (Abadie & Waroquier, independent authors with positive and null studies). Wilson and Schooler (1991<!--ref:wilson1991thinking--><!--anchor:quote:Analyzing%20reasons%20can%20focus%20people%27s%20attention%20on%20nonoptimal%20criteria%2C%20causing%20them%20to%20base%20their%20subsequent%20choices%20on%20these%20criteria.-->) remain the strongest evidence that deliberation can harm, since "Analyzing reasons can focus people's attention on nonoptimal criteria", but with small samples, a preference criterion and no located replication, "thinking too much" is rated Emerging.


#### Theme 8. Rapid social perception and deliberate holistic combination: different constructs

**Evidence strength: Moderate for the thin-slice half (Level I with imperfect construct mapping; cross-level convergence rests on a † entry); Strong for the clinical-versus-mechanical half (two Level I, Grade A meta-analyses)** (four sources, Levels I and III).

Thin slices of behaviour under five minutes predict interpersonal outcomes at a mean r of .39 (source notes), and "Studies using longer periods of behavioral observation did not yield greater predictive accuracy" (Ambady & Rosenthal, 1992<!--ref:ambady1992thin--><!--anchor:quote:Studies%20using%20longer%20periods%20of%20behavioral%20observation%20did%20not%20yield%20greater%20predictive%20accuracy-->). Three caveats travel with it: the anchor's provenance is indirect; several criteria are consensus ratings rather than ground truth, which the Phase 1 accuracy definition permits; and the 1992 bias-correction methods are dated. Coded one-minute slices show "some loss in predictive validity with 1-min slices, but relatively little loss when Slices 1 and 2 were combined" (Murphy et al., 2019†<!--ref:murphy2019predictive--><!--anchor:quote:Results%20indicate%20some%20loss%20in%20predictive%20validity%20with%201-min%20slices%2C%20but%20relatively%20little%20loss%20when%20Slices%201%20and%202%20were%20combined-->), bounding the information available, not the judgment. This half is Moderate rather than Strong because the consensus-criteria caveat is an imperfect construct mapping under Section 3.4, and its only cross-level convergence is a † entry.

At the same level, "mechanical-prediction techniques were about 10% more accurate than clinical predictions" (Grove et al., 2000<!--ref:grove2000clinical--><!--anchor:quote:On%20average%2C%20mechanical-prediction%20techniques%20were%20about%2010%25%20more%20accurate%20than%20clinical%20predictions.-->), and holistic combination lost validity even for knowledgeable experts (Kuncel et al., 2013<!--ref:kuncel2013mechanical--><!--anchor:quote:There%20was%20consistent%20and%20substantial%20loss%20of%20validity%20when%20data%20were%20combined%20holistically%E2%80%94even%20by%20experts%20who%20are%20knowledgeable%20about%20the%20jobs%20and%20organizations%20in%20question-->); "clinical judgment" there conflates intuition with deliberation, absolute effects are small, and many primary studies are older forensic predictions. The halves are compatible once Table 1 is applied: thin-slice accuracy is rapid pattern recognition over a regular social cue domain, clinical-versus-mechanical is deliberate holistic combination in long-horizon prediction; the gut may be a good cue detector and a poor cue combiner. The reconciliation is provisional: Betsch and Glöckner (2010), excluded only for lack of an anchor, hold that intuition is effortless integration of much information, which would call for a theory of intuitive integration rather than construct separation.


#### Theme 9. Confidence and the feeling of knowing are not reliably diagnostic

**Evidence strength: Moderate** (seven sources, Levels II to VII).

"Subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgment accuracy" (Kahneman & Klein, 2009<!--ref:kahneman2009conditions--><!--anchor:quote:Subjective%20experience%20is%20not%20a%20reliable%20indicator%20of%20judgment%20accuracy.-->): not reliable, not uninformative. Confidence fails in wicked settings, as in Tetlock's (2005<!--ref:tetlock2005expert--><!--anchor:quote:is%20better%20able%20to%20improvise%20in%20response%20to%20changing%20events%E2%80%94is%20more%20successful%20in%20predicting%20the%20future%20than%20the%20hedgehog-->) deliberate forecasters, and feelings are inflated by irrelevant cues: fluency and affect (Topolinski & Strack, 2009<!--ref:topolinski2009architecture--><!--anchor:quote:high%20fluency%20and%20positive%20affect%20independently%20and%20additively%20increased%20the%20probability%20that%20triads%20would%20be%20judged%20as%20coherent%2C%20irrespective%20of%20actual%20coherence-->), familiarity without a veridical object (Brown, 2003<!--ref:brown2003review--><!--anchor:quote:About%2060%25%20of%20the%20population%20has%20experienced%20d%C3%A9j%C3%A0%20vu%2C%20and%20its%20frequency%20decreases%20with%20age.-->), and bodily signals that strengthen whatever choice they favour (Dunn et al., 2010<!--ref:dunn2010listening--><!--anchor:quote:increasing%20interoception%20ability%20either%20helped%20or%20hindered%20adaptive%20intuitive%20decision%20making%2C%20depending%20on%20whether%20the%20anticipatory%20bodily%20signals%20generated%20favored%20advantageous%20or%20disadvantageous%20choices-->). But confidence also tracks accuracy where cues are valid: the feeling of knowing is accurate to the extent that accessible partial information is correct (Koriat, 1993<!--ref:koriat1993how--><!--anchor:quote:The%20results%20challenge%20the%20view%20that%20FOK%20is%20based%20on%20a%20direct%2C%20privileged%20access%20to%20an%20internal%20monitor.-->), and nonconscious emotional information that improved accuracy raised confidence with it (Lufityanto et al., 2016<!--ref:lufityanto2016measuring--><!--anchor:quote:nonconscious%20emotional%20information%20can%20boost%20accuracy%20and%20confidence%20in%20a%20concurrent%20emotion-free%20decision%20task%2C%20while%20also%20speeding%20up%20response%20times-->). The confidence-accuracy relation is thus an output of the learning environment; the strength of a gut feeling cannot by itself tell the judge which case applies (Themes 5 and 6 supply the external check).

### 4.3 Sub-question 3: The sixth-sense reading


#### Theme 10. The anomalous-cognition reading against the pre-registered, replicated, robust standard

**Evidence strength: Strong (against) for Bem's Experiments 1, 8 and 9; unreplicated in this corpus for Experiments 2 to 7; Emerging (against) for physiological presentiment, which is untested on the Phase 1 standard** (fifteen sources, Levels I, II, V and VII).

**What proponents claim.** Bem (2011<!--ref:bem2011feeling--><!--anchor:quote:The%20mean%20effect%20size%20%28d%29%20in%20psi%20performance%20across%20all%209%20experiments%20was%200.22%2C%20and%20all%20but%20one%20of%20the%20experiments%20yielded%20statistically%20significant%20results.-->) reported a mean d of 0.22 across nine experiments, "all but one" significant, with one-tailed tests and no pre-registration. Bem et al. (2016<!--ref:bem2016feeling--><!--anchor:quote:the%20combined%20effect%20size%20for%20replications%20by%20independent%20investigators%20is%200.06%2C%20z%20%3D%204.16%2C%20p%20%3D%201.1%20%C3%97%2010%E2%81%BB%E2%81%B5-->) pooled 90 experiments from 33 laboratories (g = 0.09 overall; source notes), found that "the combined effect size for replications by independent investigators is 0.06", and reported larger effects for implicit, fast-thinking protocols, which is why the evidence is called proponent-curated rather than author-dependent. Mossbridge et al. (2012<!--ref:mossbridge2012predictive--><!--anchor:quote:Higher%20quality%20experiments%20produced%20a%20quantitatively%20larger%20effect%20size%20and%20a%20greater%20level%20of%20significance%20than%20lower%20quality%20studies.-->) report a presentiment effect of 0.21 across 26 reports (source notes); Cardeña (2018<!--ref:cardena2018experimental--><!--anchor:quote:The%20evidence%20provides%20cumulative%20support%20for%20the%20reality%20of%20psi%2C%20which%20cannot%20be%20readily%20explained%20away%20by%20the%20quality%20of%20the%20studies%2C%20fraud%2C%20selective%20reporting-->) reads the record as "cumulative support for the reality of psi".

**What the critiques showed.** A default Bayesian reanalysis found the evidence "weak to nonexistent" (Wagenmakers et al., 2011<!--ref:wagenmakers2011why--><!--anchor:quote:We%20reanalyze%20Bem%27s%20data%20with%20a%20default%20Bayesian%20t%20test%20and%20show%20that%20the%20evidence%20for%20psi%20is%20weak%20to%20nonexistent.-->); even the more favourable pooled Bayes factor, at most about 40 (source notes), is "orders of magnitude lower than what is required to overcome appropriate skepticism of ESP" (Rouder & Morey, 2011<!--ref:rouder2011bayes--><!--anchor:quote:we%20believe%20it%20is%20orders%20of%20magnitude%20lower%20than%20what%20is%20required%20to%20overcome%20appropriate%20skepticism%20of%20ESP-->; a pooling of one paper's experiments). Undisclosed analytic flexibility "dramatically increases actual false-positive rates", enough to produce d near 0.2 with no true effect (Simmons et al., 2011<!--ref:simmons2011false--><!--anchor:quote:flexibility%20in%20data%20collection%2C%20analysis%2C%20and%20reporting%20dramatically%20increases%20actual%20false-positive%20rates-->). For presentiment, Schwarzkopf (2014<!--ref:schwarzkopf2014should--><!--anchor:quote:the%20seismic%20nature%20of%20these%20claims%20cannot%20be%20overstated%3A%20future%20events%20influencing%20the%20past%20breaks%20the%20second%20law%20of%20thermodynamics-->; Grade C opinion) argues that flexible analysis windows and expectation bias suffice. The proponents themselves name "expectation bias and multiple analyses" as the hardest validity threats (Mossbridge et al., 2014<!--ref:mossbridge2014predicting--><!--anchor:quote:examines%20the%20two%20most%20difficult%20challenges%20for%20obtaining%20valid%20evidence%20for%20it%3A%20expectation%20bias%20and%20multiple%20analyses-->).

**What the pre-registered and mega-scale replications found.** Three pre-registered replications of Experiment 9 failed (combined n = 150, p = .83; Ritchie et al., 2012<!--ref:ritchie2012failing--><!--anchor:quote:All%20three%20replication%20attempts%20failed%20to%20produce%20significant%20effects%20%28combined%20n%20%3D%20150%3B%20combined%20p%20%3D%20.83%2C%20one-tailed%29-->), at power near 0.5 to 0.6, so not decisive alone. Seven replications of Experiments 8 and 9 (N = 3,289; two pre-registered, several online) found d = 0.04, "no different from 0" (Galak et al., 2012<!--ref:galak2012correcting--><!--anchor:quote:find%20that%20the%20average%20effect%20size%20%28d%20%3D%200.04%29%20is%20no%20different%20from%200-->). An adversarially co-designed Registered Report on Experiment 1 "found 49.89% successful guesses, while Bem reported 53.07%" (Kekecs et al., 2023<!--ref:kekecs2023raising--><!--anchor:quote:We%20found%2049.89%25%20successful%20guesses%2C%20while%20Bem%20reported%2053.07%25%20success%20rate%2C%20with%20the%20chance%20level%20being%2050%25.-->; cited with its correction notice, Section 3.5). Three further pre-registered studies on Experiment 1 (26,483 participants) found Study 1 null, Study 2 a confirmatory below-chance effect (49.65%, p = .013) and Study 3 null (50.07%); the authors judged a method-derived false positive "more plausible" (Walleczek et al., 2025<!--ref:walleczek2025metascientific--><!--anchor:quote:Using%20conventional%20standards%2C%20based%20on%20the%20lack%20of%20replicability%20in%20Study%203%20and%20absence%20of%20an%20accepted%20scientific%20theory%2C%20the%20second%20scenario%20appears%20more%20plausible.-->). The source notes give N = 127,000 for Study 2, which against 26,483 participants can only be a trial count (unverified against the full text). Study 2, pre-registered and not analytically flexible, is set aside because the standard that rejects Bem's unreplicated positives rejects an unreplicated negative. For presentiment, Siller et al. (2015<!--ref:siller2015investigating--><!--anchor:quote:We%20could%20not%20find%20any%20evidence%20of%20presentiment%20but%20did%20find%20evidence%20of%20physiological%20correlates%20of%20expectation.-->) "could not find any evidence of presentiment but did find evidence of physiological correlates of expectation"; the design departs from classical protocols and was not pre-registered, so by the authors' own statement it cannot settle whether earlier positives were confounds.

**Adjudication on the Phase 1 standard.** Where the standard has been applied, to Experiments 1, 8 and 9, the claim fails all three criteria, and the null is supported by Grade A and B sources against Grade C proponent sources. Experiments 2 to 7 (priming, habituation, approach-avoidance) have no located replication in this corpus; Muhmenthaler et al. (2024; three Bem paradigms, more than 2,000 participants, null, not pre-registered) was held back under the cap and would widen coverage. Physiological presentiment has not been tested on the standard at all: its disconfirmation rests on one non-pre-registered Level II experiment that cannot by design settle the question, a Grade C opinion piece, and the proponents' own concession about confounds. Duggan and Tressoldi (2018; 27 new experiments, effect size 0.28, held back) is the item most likely to move this row, and it is the label most sensitive to venue inclusion. The row is Emerging (against) rather than Unresolved because the burden sits with the positive claim: the proponent meta-analyses are proponent-run and not pre-registered, an ordinary explanation has been shown in principle, and the proponents concede the two confounds. The verdict is therefore scoped: the sixth-sense reading does not meet the standard where tested, and is untested rather than refuted for presentiment. Because parapsychology-field journals were excluded by design, this is a mainstream-venue verdict; through the multi-site mega-studies it is also the most culturally general finding in the report. The a priori positions, that "Claims made by parapsychologists cannot be true" (Reber & Alcock, 2020<!--ref:reber2020searching--><!--anchor:quote:Claims%20made%20by%20parapsychologists%20cannot%20be%20true.%20The%20effects%20reported%20can%20have%20no%20ontological%20status%3B%20the%20data%20have%20no%20existential%20value.-->) and Cardeña's inventory on the other side, are Level VII with mirror-image stakes; this report relies on neither. The proponent replies to the critiques are stated and answered in Section 5.3.

#### Theme 11. Ordinary-cognition explanations of premonition experiences

**Evidence strength: Moderate** (five sources, Levels II, IV and V).

Why an experience feels like a sixth sense has answers independent of whether psi exists. Believers made more probability errors and showed a chance baseline shift that "could underlie the illusion of control and the belief in psi", while "No evidence of psi was found" (Blackmore & Trościanko, 1985<!--ref:blackmore1985belief--><!--anchor:quote:This%20%27chance%20baseline%20shift%27%20could%20underlie%20the%20illusion%20of%20control%20and%20the%20belief%20in%20psi.%20No%20evidence%20of%20psi%20was%20found.-->; small N, mixed replications). Probability misjudgement, fantasy proneness and correspondence-finding lead people to misattribute "paranormal causation to experiences that have a normal explanation" (Wiseman & Watt, 2006<!--ref:wiseman2006belief--><!--anchor:quote:increase%20the%20likelihood%20of%20them%20misattributing%20paranormal%20causation%20to%20experiences%20that%20have%20a%20normal%20explanation-->; inconsistent, correlational evidence). That the body anticipates the recent sequence, not the future, is shown in principle by Siller et al. (2015<!--ref:siller2015investigating--><!--anchor:quote:We%20could%20not%20find%20any%20evidence%20of%20presentiment%20but%20did%20find%20evidence%20of%20physiological%20correlates%20of%20expectation.-->). With Themes 1, 3 and 4 (Brown, 2003<!--ref:brown2003review--><!--anchor:quote:About%2060%25%20of%20the%20population%20has%20experienced%20d%C3%A9j%C3%A0%20vu%2C%20and%20its%20frequency%20decreases%20with%20age.-->; Koriat, 1993<!--ref:koriat1993how--><!--anchor:quote:The%20results%20challenge%20the%20view%20that%20FOK%20is%20based%20on%20a%20direct%2C%20privileged%20access%20to%20an%20internal%20monitor.-->), the ordinary account predicts the phenomenology the sixth-sense reading takes as evidence: a feeling arrives, its basis is not reportable, and hits are remembered. Moderate because the belief literature is small-sample and mixed, and mainstream research on premonition base rates is thin.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Interpretation and theoretical implications

Across the three ordinary constructs the answer is a single framework: implicit learning supplies the content, fluency, affect and bodily state supply the signal that content has been matched, and whether that signal was ever calibrated against truth depends on the learning history. No study in the corpus coded environment kindness ex ante and measured gut-feeling accuracy as the outcome, so this is a framework, not a finding. The sceptic's simpler story deserves equal statement: fast judgments are accurate exactly when the underlying knowledge is accurate, and "environment kindness" describes that knowledge's provenance rather than an additional mechanism. On this corpus the two accounts are not distinguishable; the ex ante operationalisation of Hogarth et al. (2015<!--ref:hogarth2015two--><!--anchor:quote:Kind%20learning%20environments%20involve%20close%20matches%20between%20the%20informational%20elements%20in%20the%20two%20settings%20and%20are%20a%20necessary%20condition%20for%20accurate%20inferences.-->) is what would allow them to be.

The corpus supports a dual-process vocabulary but not the default-interventionist story: parallel-competitive formulations (Hodgkinson & Sadler-Smith, 2018†<!--ref:hodgkinson2018dynamics--><!--anchor:quote:parallel-competitive%20formulations%20offer%20a%20more%20nuanced%20and%20realistic%20depiction%20of%20organizational%20decision%20makers%20as%20thinking%20and%20feeling%20beings-->), knowledge-centred clinical reasoning (Norman et al., 2024†<!--ref:norman2024dual--><!--anchor:quote:they%20arise%20from%20lack%20of%20access%20to%20the%20appropriate%20knowledge%2C%20not%20from%20errors%20of%20processing-->) and the memory-representation account of unconscious thought (Abadie & Waroquier, 2019†<!--ref:abadie2019evaluating--><!--anchor:quote:a%20distraction%20period%20is%20more%20useful%20when%20meaning-based%20gist%20representations%20of%20the%20alternatives%20are%20accessible-->) agree that what is in memory predicts accuracy better than which process ran, and Hu et al. (2025†<!--ref:hu2025intuition--><!--anchor:quote:individuals%20with%20high%20CRT%20scores%20had%20superior%20performance%20and%20relied%20more%20on%20intuition-->) add a single-lab, model-based observation that "individuals with high CRT scores had superior performance and relied more on intuition", intuition there being a drift-diffusion starting-point bias. This answers the Checkpoint 1 counter-argument that gut feelings are only fast cognition with confabulated introspection: the corpus supports not a separate knowledge channel but a distinct output format, a judgment delivered with a feeling and without its reasons; the feeling is a lawful inference (Koriat, 1993<!--ref:koriat1993how--><!--anchor:quote:The%20results%20challenge%20the%20view%20that%20FOK%20is%20based%20on%20a%20direct%2C%20privileged%20access%20to%20an%20internal%20monitor.-->), not noise, but not evidence in its own right.

The somatic marker hypothesis survives only in weak form: bodily signals bias choice in the direction they point (Dunn et al., 2010<!--ref:dunn2010listening--><!--anchor:quote:increasing%20interoception%20ability%20either%20helped%20or%20hindered%20adaptive%20intuitive%20decision%20making%2C%20depending%20on%20whether%20the%20anticipatory%20bodily%20signals%20generated%20favored%20advantageous%20or%20disadvantageous%20choices-->) without evidence that they carry information the person lacks consciously (Maia & McClelland, 2004<!--ref:maia2004reexamination--><!--anchor:quote:participants%20report%20knowledge%20of%20the%20advantageous%20strategy%20more%20reliably%20than%20they%20behave%20advantageously-->; Dunn et al., 2006<!--ref:dunn2006somatic--><!--anchor:quote:their%20interpretation%20is%20undermined%20by%20the%20cognitive%20penetrability%20of%20the%20reward/punishment%20schedule%2C%20ambiguity%20surrounding%20interpretation%20of%20the%20psychophysiological%20data%2C%20and%20a%20shortage%20of%20causal%20evidence-->). [MATERIAL GAP: no annotated source supports a claim about interoceptive inference or predictive processing; a targeted follow-up search is recommended rather than filling the gap from memory.]


### 5.2 Practical implications

Every recommendation here is bounded by evidence that is framework-level for the boundary conditions and primary-level only for coherence intuitions, the unconscious-thought null, thin-slice validity and the precognition null.

**When a gut feeling warrants trust.** An environment regular enough to be learned, and sustained experience with valid feedback in it: kind environments are "a necessary condition for accurate inferences" (Hogarth et al., 2015<!--ref:hogarth2015two--><!--anchor:quote:Kind%20learning%20environments%20involve%20close%20matches%20between%20the%20informational%20elements%20in%20the%20two%20settings%20and%20are%20a%20necessary%20condition%20for%20accurate%20inferences.-->), and both traditions converged on these conditions while agreeing that confidence does not certify them (Kahneman & Klein, 2009<!--ref:kahneman2009conditions--><!--anchor:quote:Subjective%20experience%20is%20not%20a%20reliable%20indicator%20of%20judgment%20accuracy.-->). Intuition beat instructed analysis for experts in one Grade C, unreplicated test (Dane et al., 2012<!--ref:dane2012trust--><!--anchor:quote:the%20effectiveness%20of%20intuition%20relative%20to%20analysis%20is%20amplified%20at%20a%20high%20level%20of%20domain%20expertise-->); rapid impressions predict interpersonal outcomes (Ambady & Rosenthal, 1992<!--ref:ambady1992thin--><!--anchor:quote:Studies%20using%20longer%20periods%20of%20behavioral%20observation%20did%20not%20yield%20greater%20predictive%20accuracy-->), several criteria being consensus ratings.

**When it does not.** Where feedback is delayed, missing or misleading (Hogarth, 2001<!--ref:hogarth2001educating--><!--anchor:quote:intuition%20is%20a%20normal%20and%20important%20component%20of%20thought%20that%20has%20its%20roots%20in%20processes%20of%20tacit%20learning-->); where the feeling came from fluency or mood, which awareness does not correct (Topolinski & Strack, 2009<!--ref:topolinski2009architecture--><!--anchor:quote:high%20fluency%20and%20positive%20affect%20independently%20and%20additively%20increased%20the%20probability%20that%20triads%20would%20be%20judged%20as%20coherent%2C%20irrespective%20of%20actual%20coherence-->); where a bodily signal was not shaped by valid outcomes (Dunn et al., 2010<!--ref:dunn2010listening--><!--anchor:quote:increasing%20interoception%20ability%20either%20helped%20or%20hindered%20adaptive%20intuitive%20decision%20making%2C%20depending%20on%20whether%20the%20anticipatory%20bodily%20signals%20generated%20favored%20advantageous%20or%20disadvantageous%20choices-->); and in long-horizon prediction from decomposable data, where a mechanical rule beats deliberate holistic combination even by experts (Grove et al., 2000<!--ref:grove2000clinical--><!--anchor:quote:On%20average%2C%20mechanical-prediction%20techniques%20were%20about%2010%25%20more%20accurate%20than%20clinical%20predictions.-->; Kuncel et al., 2013<!--ref:kuncel2013mechanical--><!--anchor:quote:There%20was%20consistent%20and%20substantial%20loss%20of%20validity%20when%20data%20were%20combined%20holistically%E2%80%94even%20by%20experts%20who%20are%20knowledgeable%20about%20the%20jobs%20and%20organizations%20in%20question-->), a neighbouring construct on which no corpus evidence shows gut feelings doing better. Confidence cannot indicate which situation applies (Kahneman & Klein, 2009<!--ref:kahneman2009conditions--><!--anchor:quote:Subjective%20experience%20is%20not%20a%20reliable%20indicator%20of%20judgment%20accuracy.-->).

**How to make a gut feeling trustworthy.** The levers lie in the learning history: kind environments with prompt, accurate feedback (Hogarth et al., 2015<!--ref:hogarth2015two--><!--anchor:quote:Kind%20learning%20environments%20involve%20close%20matches%20between%20the%20informational%20elements%20in%20the%20two%20settings%20and%20are%20a%20necessary%20condition%20for%20accurate%20inferences.-->) and accumulated knowledge, since error falls with knowledge (Norman et al., 2017<!--ref:norman2017causes--><!--anchor:quote:with%20increasing%20expertise%20%28and%20knowledge%29%2C%20the%20likelihood%20of%20errors%20decreases-->), not with instructions to slow down (Theme 1). Withholding attention does not help (Nieuwenstein et al., 2015<!--ref:nieuwenstein2015making--><!--anchor:quote:the%20large-scale%20replication%20study%20yielded%20no%20evidence%20for%20the%20UTA%2C%20and%20the%20meta-analysis%20showed%20that%20previous%20reports%20of%20the%20UTA%20were%20confined%20to%20underpowered%20studies-->); distraction helps only when gist is what the task needs (Abadie & Waroquier, 2019†<!--ref:abadie2019evaluating--><!--anchor:quote:a%20distraction%20period%20is%20more%20useful%20when%20meaning-based%20gist%20representations%20of%20the%20alternatives%20are%20accessible-->). Use an external check on the environment, not the strength of the feeling. None of these levers has been tested as an intervention in this corpus.


### 5.3 The "sixth sense" verdict

The Phase 1 standard asks for evidence that is pre-registered, independently replicated and robust to analytic flexibility; the proponent case is set out in Theme 10. Proponents reply to the critiques in three ways, the first two recorded in the pipeline's source notes rather than traced to a corpus anchor. First, sceptical experimenters suppress the effect, raised against Galak et al. (2012<!--ref:galak2012correcting--><!--anchor:quote:find%20that%20the%20average%20effect%20size%20%28d%20%3D%200.04%29%20is%20no%20different%20from%200-->) and in Bem et al. (2016<!--ref:bem2016feeling--><!--anchor:quote:the%20combined%20effect%20size%20for%20replications%20by%20independent%20investigators%20is%200.06%2C%20z%20%3D%204.16%2C%20p%20%3D%201.1%20%C3%97%2010%E2%81%BB%E2%81%B5-->). Second, psi-conducive conditions were absent in the null replications, raised against Kekecs et al. (2023<!--ref:kekecs2023raising--><!--anchor:quote:We%20found%2049.89%25%20successful%20guesses%2C%20while%20Bem%20reported%2053.07%25%20success%20rate%2C%20with%20the%20chance%20level%20being%2050%25.-->; cited with its correction notice, https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.231080, which leaves the null conclusion unaffected). ## 6. Conclusion

### 6.1 Summary and recommendations

Every conclusion here is bounded by evidence that is framework-level for the boundary conditions and primary-level only for coherence intuitions, the unconscious-thought null, thin-slice validity and the precognition null. Within that bound, gut feelings are real, can be accurate under identifiable conditions, and are explicable without a sixth sense; each ordinary construct is accurate only to the extent that its cue was shaped by valid contingencies in a regular environment. The strength labels in the Abstract and Findings carry through unchanged.

For practitioners and educators: invest in the learning environment rather than the feeling. Feedback and domain knowledge are the levers the framework identifies; none has been tested as an intervention in this corpus, and instructions to slow down or to stop thinking are not supported. For decision policy: in long-horizon prediction from decomposable data, prefer mechanical combination and treat holistic judgment, intuitive judgment included (untested for gut feelings in this corpus; Theme 6), as a hypothesis to be checked. For researchers: an ex ante test of environment kindness on gut-feeling accuracy, and a pre-registered presentiment test under the classic protocol.

### 6.2 Plain-language answer

Does the evidence support a sixth sense, a channel beyond ordinary perception, memory and learning? Where the claim has been put to the strictest test, no. The best-known claim produced small effects in studies that were not pre-registered and used analytic choices that, as later work showed, can produce effects of that size with no true effect. When its first procedure was rerun under pre-registration by mixed proponent-sceptic teams with tens of thousands of participants, and two others by independent teams with several thousand, partly under pre-registration, the effect was at chance; the other six procedures have not been retested here. The claim that the body reacts seconds before a random event rests on proponent-run meta-analyses and has an ordinary explanation shown in principle but not yet tested under the classic protocol with pre-registration: "not supported and not yet properly tested", not "ruled out". This is a mainstream-journal verdict; parapsychology's own journals were outside scope.

What the evidence does support is that gut feelings are real, can be accurate under identifiable conditions, and are explicable without a sixth sense. They are the output of pattern learning largely not put into words, though how much is truly unconscious is still debated, delivered as a feeling of coherence, familiarity or bodily pull. They are accurate when the person has had repeated experience with honest feedback in a situation with real regularities and the present case is one of those; they are unreliable, often confidently so, when feedback was delayed, missing or misleading, when the feeling came from fluency or mood, or when a simple formula beats any holistic judgment. That account is a well-supported framework rather than a tested prediction. Finally, the strength of a gut feeling is only as trustworthy as the environment that trained it; it cannot by itself tell you which kind of environment you are in.


### 6.3 Closing

The folk phrase "knowing without knowing how" describes a lawful output format of ordinary cognition rather than a hidden channel. The scientific task is not to decide whether to trust the gut but to learn, case by case and in advance, whether the environment that trained it was one in which trust is earned.
